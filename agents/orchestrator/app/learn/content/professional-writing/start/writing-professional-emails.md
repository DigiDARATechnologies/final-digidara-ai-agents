takeaways:
- A good email has a clear subject, a short opening, the main message, and a clear next step
- Subject lines decide whether the email is opened
- Keep one purpose per email, and check the To, Cc and attachments before sending

## The structure

| Part | What to write | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Subject** | what the email is about, in a few words | `Leave request: 10 to 12 March` |
| **Greeting** | polite and matched to the person | `Dear Ms Rao,` / `Hi Arun,` |
| **Opening line** | why you are writing | `I am writing to request leave from 10 to 12 March.` |
| **Body** | the details, in short paragraphs or bullets | reason, dates, how work will be covered |
| **Next step** | what happens now | `Please confirm by Friday.` |
| **Closing** | a polite ending | `Thank you,` / `Regards,` |
| **Signature** | your full name, role, phone | `Priya Nair, Trainee Engineer, 98xxxxxx10` |

## Subject lines

The subject is the most important line. It should say **what** the email is and, if needed, **what you need**.

| Weak | Strong |
| --- | --- |
| `Hello` | `Application for Python Developer role: Priya Nair` |
| `Regarding project` | `Project X: approval needed for budget by 15 March` |
| `Urgent!!!` | `Server down since 9 am: decision needed` |
| `Doubt` | `Question about Assignment 3, Question 2` |
| `Resume` | `Resume: Arun Kumar, B.E. CSE, 2026 batch` |

Avoid ALL CAPS, many exclamation marks and the word "urgent" for ordinary things. They get ignored or sent to spam.

## Greetings and closings

| Situation | Greeting | Closing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First time, formal, you know the name | `Dear Mr Sharma,` | `Yours sincerely,` or `Regards,` |
| First time, you do not know the name | `Dear Hiring Manager,` | `Yours faithfully,` or `Regards,` |
| Colleague you know | `Hi Meena,` | `Thanks,` / `Best regards,` |
| Replying in a conversation | `Hi Meena,` or none | `Thanks,` |

## Template 1: applying for a job

:::example Job application email
**Subject:** Application for Junior Data Analyst: Priya Nair

Dear Hiring Manager,

I am writing to apply for the Junior Data Analyst position advertised on your careers page. I am a final-year B.Sc. Statistics student with hands-on experience in Excel, SQL and Python from two academic projects.

In my last project I analysed 20,000 sales records and found that 18% of orders came from just three cities. I would be glad to bring the same approach to your team.

I have attached my resume. I am available for an interview at your convenience.

Thank you for your time.

Regards,
Priya Nair
Phone: 98xxxxxx10 | LinkedIn: linkedin.com/in/priya-nair
:::

Why it works: it names the role, gives one proof, and is short enough to read in 20 seconds.

## Template 2: asking for leave

:::example Leave request
**Subject:** Leave request: 10 to 12 March

Dear Mr Iyer,

I would like to request leave from 10 to 12 March (3 days) for a family function. I will finish the pending report before I leave, and Karthik has agreed to handle urgent queries.

Please let me know if this is approved.

Thank you,
Arun
:::

## Template 3: following up

:::example Polite follow-up
**Subject:** Following up: interview on 5 March

Dear Ms Rao,

Thank you for interviewing me for the Support Engineer role on 5 March. I enjoyed learning about your customer onboarding process. I wanted to ask whether there is any update on the next steps.

I remain very interested in the position. Thank you again for your time.

Regards,
Divya
:::

Wait **5 to 7 working days** before following up unless they gave a date.

## Template 4: saying no, or giving bad news

:::example Declining politely
**Subject:** Unable to take on the extra task this week

Hi Rahul,

Thank you for thinking of me. I cannot take on the report this week because I am finishing the client demo due on Thursday. I can start on Monday, or I can suggest someone who may have time. Which would you prefer?

Thanks,
Meena
:::

Notice the pattern: **thanks, reason, alternative.**

## Mistakes to avoid

| Mistake | Better |
| --- | --- |
| a long block of text | short paragraphs and bullets |
| typing in all lowercase or with SMS words (`pls`, `u`, `r`) | full words, proper capital letters |
| using "Reply All" for everyone | reply only to people who need it |
| forgetting the attachment | mention it in the text, then check before sending |
| a casual email address (`cutegirl123@...`) | a simple, professional address (`firstname.lastname@...`) |
| writing when angry | write it, wait an hour, read it again, then decide |
| asking three unrelated things | one email, one purpose (or numbered points) |

:::warning
**To, Cc and Bcc.** *To* is for people who must act. *Cc* is for people who should know. *Bcc* hides recipients (use it for sending to many outsiders, so you do not share their addresses). Check the recipient list before sending: the wrong "Reply All" is a classic embarrassing mistake.
:::
