## Your writing is your first impression

Before anyone meets you in an interview or a project, they usually read you: your resume, your application email, your LinkedIn message, your first reply to a client. Managers say the same thing again and again: **people who write clearly get things done faster, and people notice.**

| Situation | What clear writing gives you |
| --- | --- |
| Applying for a job | your email gets opened and your resume gets read |
| Working in a team | fewer misunderstandings and fewer "can you explain again?" calls |
| Working with clients | trust, because you sound organised |
| Asking for help | you get the answer the first time |
| Asking for a raise or promotion | a case that is easy to say yes to |

## The five rules

| Rule | What it means | Weak | Strong |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **1. Know your goal** | decide what you want the reader to *do* or *know* before you write | "Just wanted to touch base regarding the thing." | "Please approve the leave dates below by Friday." |
| **2. Know your reader** | adjust the words to their role, time and knowledge | technical jargon to a customer | plain words and one example |
| **3. Be clear** | one idea per sentence, simple words | "We shall endeavour to ascertain the feasibility" | "We will check if it is possible" |
| **4. Be brief** | cut every word that does not help | "At this point in time" | "Now" |
| **5. Be polite and professional** | respectful tone, no anger, no slang | "Why did you not reply??" | "Could you share an update when you have a moment?" |

## Put the important thing first

Busy readers decide in the first two lines whether to keep reading. Start with the **result, the request or the decision**, then add the reasons.

:::example Before and after
**Before:** "Hope you are doing well. I was going through the project plan yesterday and, after checking with a few people, I realised that there might be an issue with the deadline because the testing team is on leave next week, so I was wondering if maybe we could possibly think about moving the date."

**After:** "Can we move the deadline from 12 March to 19 March? The testing team is on leave next week, and testing needs five days."
:::

The second version gives the request and the reason in two short sentences.

## A quick self-check before you press Send

1. Can the reader tell **what I want** in the first two lines?
2. Is there anything they must **do**, and by **when**?
3. Did I remove words that add nothing?
4. Is the tone one I would be happy to see forwarded to my manager?
5. Did I check **spelling, names and numbers**?

:::tip
Read your message **aloud** before sending. If you run out of breath in a sentence, it is too long. If it sounds rude aloud, it will sound ruder on screen.
:::

:::practice communication-coach
Paste a real email you have written into the Communication Coach and ask it to point out unclear or too-long sentences.
:::
