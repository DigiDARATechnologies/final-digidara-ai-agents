## Choosing the channel

| Use | When | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Chat** (Teams, Slack, WhatsApp) | a quick question, a short update, anything that needs a fast answer | "Is the file on the shared drive?" |
| **Email** | formal, long, or needs a record; messages to people outside your team | job applications, client communication, approvals |
| **Call or video** | complex, emotional or sensitive topics; a long thread going back and forth | misunderstandings, feedback, bad news |
| **In person** | important conversations where relationships matter | resignations, serious disagreements |

If a chat has gone back and forth **more than five or six times** and is still not resolved, switch to a call.

## Chat habits that make people want to work with you

**1. Write complete messages, not "Hi" alone.**

| Weak | Strong |
| --- | --- |
| `Hi` ... (waits) | `Hi Anil, quick question: where can I find the template for the weekly report?` |
| `Are you there?` | `When you are free, could you check my pull request? It is small, about 20 lines.` |

Sending only "Hi" forces the other person to wait and reply before they know what you want. Put the question in the first message.

**2. Use threads and replies**, so conversations stay together.

**3. Respect time and time zones.** Avoid messaging colleagues late at night or on holidays unless it is truly urgent. Mention the **deadline** you need.

**4. Be clear about urgency.** `By 5 pm today` is clearer than `ASAP`.

**5. Confirm you got a request.** A quick `Got it, I'll send it by 3 pm` removes worry.

**6. Keep the tone friendly but professional.** Emojis are fine in moderation inside a team; avoid them in messages to clients or senior managers you do not know.

## WhatsApp for work

Many Indian workplaces and colleges use WhatsApp groups. Follow a few rules:

| Do | Do not |
| --- | --- |
| keep group messages relevant | forward jokes, "good morning" images or unverified news in work groups |
| use a clear profile photo and name | use an unclear or inappropriate photo |
| reply politely and within working hours | send voice notes for long, complex information that is better in writing |
| keep sensitive data out | share passwords, OTPs or confidential documents in a casual group |

## LinkedIn messages

When you message a person you do not know (a recruiter, an alumnus), be **short, specific and respectful**.

:::example Message to an alumnus
Hello Mr Das, I am a final-year Computer Science student at XYZ College. I saw that you work as a Data Engineer at ABC Ltd and are an alumnus of our college. I am preparing for data roles and would be grateful for 10 minutes of your advice on how to build a strong profile. Thank you for considering it.
:::

Why it works: it says who you are, why you are writing this person, and asks for **something small**. Do not ask a stranger "Can you give me a job?" in your first message.

## Your online presence

Recruiters search for candidates online. Keep these in good shape:

| Item | Good practice |
| --- | --- |
| **LinkedIn profile** | a clear photo, a headline that says what you do ("Final-year B.Tech student | Python | SQL"), a short summary, your projects |
| **GitHub** | a tidy profile and a few well-described projects (see the Projects section) |
| **Email address and username** | simple and professional |
| **Social media** | assume employers can see public posts; remove content you would not show an interviewer |
| **Voicemail and caller tune** | a clear, polite message |

## Video meeting etiquette

| Before | During | After |
| --- | --- | --- |
| join 2 to 5 minutes early; test audio and camera | mute when not speaking; camera on if expected | send a short summary of what was agreed |
| a quiet place and a neutral background | look at the camera, not only at the screen | do what you promised, by the date you said |
| charge your laptop; keep a backup connection | do not multitask: people can tell | thank the host |

:::warning
Never type something in a chat or group that you would not be comfortable seeing printed and read aloud. Screenshots travel, and "deleted" messages are not always really deleted.
:::

:::practice communication-coach
Practise rewriting a few of your own chat messages so that they have a clear request and a deadline.
:::
