## Why it matters

"Tell me about yourself" is the **first** question in most interviews, and "Hello, I am ..." is how every conversation starts. A prepared introduction sets a confident tone for everything that follows.

## A simple structure: Present, Past, Future

| Part | What to say | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Present** | who you are now: name, degree, college, main skill | "I am Priya Nair, a final-year B.Sc. Statistics student at St. Joseph's College." |
| **Past** | two or three highlights that prove it: a project, an internship, an achievement | "I analysed 20,000 sales records in my last project and found that three cities produced almost a fifth of all orders." |
| **Future** | what you want and why this role or this listener | "I am looking for a data analyst role where I can keep learning, and your company's focus on retail analytics is exactly what interests me." |

Start with the part the listener cares about most. For an interview, link your **skills** to the **role**.

## The 30-second version (about 80 words)

:::example Short introduction
"Good morning. I am Priya Nair, a final-year B.Sc. Statistics student from Chennai. I enjoy working with data. In my last project I analysed 20,000 sales records using Python and SQL and found that three cities produced 18% of orders. I am looking for a junior data analyst role where I can apply these skills and keep learning, and I am excited about this opportunity."
:::

## The 2-minute version

Add a bit more detail on each part: one more project or an internship, a skill you are proud of, a leadership example, and a line on your interests that shows your personality.

:::example Longer introduction
"Good morning, and thank you for the opportunity. I am Arun Kumar, a final-year B.E. Computer Science student from Coimbatore.

I have always liked solving practical problems with code. In my third year I built a Flask web application for my department that lets students book lab slots; about 120 students now use it every week, and it reduced booking conflicts that used to be handled on paper. I also completed a three-month Python internship at a local software company, where I wrote scripts that automated a daily report and saved the team about an hour a day.

Outside studies I captain our college cricket team, which has taught me to work with people and stay calm under pressure.

I am now looking for a software developer role where I can learn from experienced engineers and contribute to real products. Your work on education technology really interests me, because I have seen how much a simple tool can help students."
:::

## Make it your own

| Do | Do not |
| --- | --- |
| use specific facts and numbers | list adjectives ("I am hard-working, sincere, dedicated") with nothing to prove them |
| speak naturally, as you would to a person | read it like a memorised speech |
| connect it to the listener's needs | recite your whole resume |
| smile and make eye contact | start with "Myself Arun..." (say "I am Arun" or "My name is Arun") |
| end with what you want | end with "That's all" or trail off |
| keep the personal part short (family, hobbies) unless asked | start with your father's occupation or date of birth |

## Adapt for different situations

| Situation | Focus |
| --- | --- |
| **Job interview** | skills and results that match the role |
| **College placement round** | academics, projects, internships, your interest in the company |
| **Networking event** | who you are, what you are exploring, one question for the person |
| **First day at a new job or class** | your name, background in one line, something friendly |
| **Email or LinkedIn** | two or three lines, with a clear ask |

## Practise

1. Write your introduction as **bullet points**, not a paragraph.
2. Say it aloud and time it: 30 seconds, then 2 minutes.
3. Record yourself, listen, and cut anything that sounds like a cliché.
4. Practise until you can say it **naturally**, with small changes each time.
5. Prepare three versions: for an interview, for a networking event, for a new group.

:::practice mock-interview
Run a mock interview and begin with "Tell me about yourself". Replay your answer and check it against the checklist above.
:::
