## What slides are for

Slides should **help the audience understand and remember**. They are not a teleprompter, and they are not a handout. If people are reading your slide, they are not listening to you.

| Bad slide | Good slide |
| --- | --- |
| 15 lines of small text | a title and 3 short bullets or one picture |
| the speaker reads every word | the speaker adds explanation and examples |
| every effect and animation | simple, consistent design |
| clip art and a cluttered background | one strong photo or diagram |

## One idea per slide

If you have to say "also" on a slide, you probably need two slides.

| Rule | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| **One main idea** per slide | the title states it |
| **Title as the message** | "Sales rose 18% in March", not "Sales Data" |
| **6 words per line, 6 lines at most** (a guide, not a law) | fewer is better |
| **Font size** | at least 24 pt for text, 32 pt or more for titles |
| **Plain fonts** | Calibri, Arial, Helvetica; one or two fonts in total |
| **High contrast** | dark text on a light background, or white on dark; never light grey on white |
| **Consistent style** | same colours, fonts and layout throughout |

## Visuals

| Use | Instead of |
| --- | --- |
| a simple diagram | a paragraph describing the process |
| a real photo | generic clip art |
| a chart with one clear message | a table of 100 numbers |
| an icon or a number in a very large font | five bullets about the same thing |

For example, rather than three bullets about growth, show **"+18%"** in huge type with one line under it.

## Charts and data

| Good practice | Why |
| --- | --- |
| choose the right chart (bars to compare, lines for trends, pie for 2 to 4 parts of a whole) | the audience reads it correctly |
| start bar charts at zero | otherwise small differences look huge |
| label axes and units; give the source | credibility |
| remove clutter: gridlines, 3-D effects, heavy borders | focus |
| highlight the one bar or line that matters | guides the eye |
| write the **takeaway** as the slide title | people do not have to work it out |

## A simple slide structure for a project talk

| Slide | Content |
| --- | --- |
| 1. Title | project name, your name, date |
| 2. The problem | why it matters, in a sentence and one number |
| 3. The solution | what you built, with one picture |
| 4. How it works | a simple diagram |
| 5. Results | the key numbers or a demo screenshot |
| 6. What I learned / challenges | honest and brief |
| 7. Next steps and thank you | what you will do next; contact details |

For a 10-minute talk, 6 to 8 slides is plenty.

## Using slides while speaking

| Do | Do not |
| --- | --- |
| face the audience; glance at the screen briefly | talk to the screen with your back to the audience |
| click, pause, then say the point (let people look first) | read the slide aloud word for word |
| use the slide as a prompt for your story | put your whole script on it |
| use a blank slide (press B) when you want attention on you | leave a confusing slide up while you talk about something else |
| have a clicker or know the keyboard shortcuts | fumble with the mouse |

## Prepare for technical problems

| Risk | Prevention |
| --- | --- |
| the laptop does not connect | bring the right adapter; arrive early to test |
| the file will not open | save as **PDF** as well as PowerPoint; keep a copy on email or cloud and a USB drive |
| fonts and images are missing | embed fonts or use standard ones |
| no internet for a live demo | record a short **video** of the demo as a backup |
| the slides crash | be ready to give the talk **without** slides: know your points |
| a different screen shape | design for 16:9 and check |

## Handouts and sharing

If you want to give people details, put them in a **separate document** or in the speaker notes, and share it afterwards. Do not cram a handout into the slides.

:::warning
Never read from slides in a presentation to faculty or an interview panel. They can read faster than you speak, and it makes you look unprepared. Use short prompts and speak from your understanding.
:::

:::tip
Test your slides on a **phone or a large screen from the back of the room**. If you cannot read them from there, the text is too small.
:::
