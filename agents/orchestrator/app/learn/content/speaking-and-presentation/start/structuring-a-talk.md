title: Structuring a talk
summary: Plan a talk from audience to ending: choose one main message, build an opening, body and close, and use stories and examples to keep people interested.
takeaways:
- Start with your audience and one main message
- Use a clear opening, three or fewer key points, and a memorable close
- Stories and examples are what people remember
---
## Start with the audience, not the slides

Before you write a single slide, answer three questions:

| Question | Example |
| --- | --- |
| **Who** is listening? | first-year students; a panel of faculty; managers |
| **What** do they already know, and what do they care about? | no coding background; mainly interested in jobs |
| **What** should they think, feel or do afterwards? | decide to join the club; approve the budget |

Decide on **one main message**, a sentence you want them to remember. If you cannot say it in one sentence, the talk is not ready.

## The three-part structure

| Part | Share of time | Job |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Opening** | 10 to 15% | get attention, show why it matters, preview what is coming |
| **Body** | 70 to 80% | deliver 2 or 3 key points, each with an example |
| **Close** | 10 to 15% | summarise and say what to do or remember |

An old rule of speaking: **tell them what you will tell them, tell them, then tell them what you told them.**

## The opening

Do not begin with "Today I will talk about..." or "I am sorry, I am not well prepared". Start with something that wakes people up:

| Opening | Example |
| --- | --- |
| a surprising fact | "Every minute, over 500 hours of video are uploaded to YouTube." |
| a question | "How many of you checked your phone in the last ten minutes?" |
| a short story | "Last year, I nearly missed my exam because of a wrong hall ticket..." |
| a bold statement | "Most resumes are rejected in under ten seconds." |
| a problem | "Our lab bookings are wasting 40 hours a week." |

Then say **who you are** (briefly), **why the topic matters to them**, and **what the talk will cover**.

## The body: three points or fewer

People remember only a few things. Limit yourself to **two or three** main points. For each:

1. **State** the point in one sentence.
2. **Prove** it with a fact, number or example.
3. **Link** it to the audience: "so what does this mean for you?"

Ways to organise the body:

| Pattern | Use for |
| --- | --- |
| **Problem, cause, solution** | project proposals, pitches |
| **Past, present, future** | company or technology overviews |
| **Step by step** | how-to talks and demos |
| **Pros and cons** | recommendations |
| **Three reasons** | persuading |

## Stories, examples, numbers

Facts alone are forgotten. **Stories** are remembered.

:::example A story makes it stick
**Flat:** "Version control is important for teams."

**Story:** "Last year our team lost two days of work because two of us edited the same file and one version overwrote the other. After that, we started using Git. Since then, we have not lost a single line of work."
:::

Keep stories **short** (30 to 60 seconds), **specific** (real names, places, numbers), and **relevant** to your point.

## The close

Do not end with "Thank you. Any questions?" alone. Close with force:

| Part | Example |
| --- | --- |
| **Summarise** the main message | "To recap: a clean resume, a clear introduction and honest answers will take you far." |
| **Call to action** | "This week, rewrite your resume bullets using the action-and-result formula." |
| **A memorable last line** | "Preparation is the answer to nerves." |
| **Thank you** | a brief, warm thanks |

Then invite questions.

## Time it

| Rule | Why |
| --- | --- |
| rehearse with a timer | most people talk longer than planned |
| plan for **less** than the time you are given | nervous speakers speed up; questions need time |
| mark the points where you can cut if you run late | never rush the ending |
| roughly **one slide per 1 to 2 minutes** | too many slides overwhelm the audience |

## Planning on one page

Write your talk as a one-page outline, not a script:

| Part | Notes |
| --- | --- |
| **Main message** | one sentence |
| **Opening** | the hook, who I am, what I will cover |
| **Point 1** | statement, example, link to the audience |
| **Point 2** | statement, example, link to the audience |
| **Point 3** | statement, example, link to the audience |
| **Close** | recap, call to action, final line |

:::tip
If you are given a topic with a very short time (a 2-minute talk, a viva), pick **one** main point, support it with **one** example, and leave time for questions. Short and clear beats long and crowded.
:::
