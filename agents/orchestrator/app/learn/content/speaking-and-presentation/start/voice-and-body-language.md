## Your voice

| Element | Common problem | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Pace** | speaking too fast when nervous | aim for about 120 to 150 words a minute; pause at the end of each idea |
| **Volume** | too soft to be heard at the back | speak to the person farthest away; breathe from your belly |
| **Pitch and tone** | flat, monotone | vary your tone; emphasise key words |
| **Clarity** | mumbling, swallowing word endings | open your mouth; finish each word; slow down |
| **Pauses** | filling every gap with "um" and "uh" | use silence on purpose |

### Pauses are powerful

A pause before an important point makes people lean in. A pause after it lets the idea sink in. Silence feels long to you and **natural** to the audience.

### Fillers

Words such as "um", "uh", "like", "you know", "basically", "actually" and "so" are normal in small amounts. Too many distract.

| To reduce them | |
| --- | --- |
| notice them | record yourself and count |
| replace the filler with a silent pause | breathe instead of saying "um" |
| slow down | fillers appear when thoughts are racing |
| prepare your first sentence | opening fillers are the most common |

## Body language

People judge confidence within seconds, mostly from how you look.

| Part | Aim for | Avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Posture** | standing tall, feet shoulder-width apart, weight balanced | slouching, leaning on the podium, swaying |
| **Hands** | open, natural gestures at waist or chest height | hands in pockets, crossed arms, fidgeting with a pen, pointing at people |
| **Face** | a natural, friendly expression; smile where it fits | frozen face; smiling during serious points |
| **Movement** | a few purposeful steps, then stop | pacing nervously |
| **Eye contact** | look at one person for a full thought (3 to 5 seconds), then another | staring at the floor, the screen or your notes the whole time |

## Eye contact, made simple

- Pick **friendly faces** in different parts of the room: left, centre, right.
- Speak a **complete sentence or idea** to one person, then move to another.
- In a video meeting, look at the **camera** when you speak (it feels odd but looks like eye contact to others).
- If direct eye contact is uncomfortable, look at the **forehead or the space between the eyebrows**.
- Culture matters: in some settings, too much direct eye contact with seniors can seem disrespectful. In most professional and interview settings in India and abroad, **steady, polite eye contact** is read as confidence and honesty.

## Entering and leaving

The first and last ten seconds are remembered.

| Moment | Do |
| --- | --- |
| **Walking in** | head up, steady pace, a calm smile; greet the panel or audience |
| **Handshake** (if offered) | firm, brief, with eye contact; right hand; not limp, not crushing |
| **Sitting** | sit upright, back against the chair, hands on the table or lap; do not slouch or tilt back |
| **Leaving** | thank them, smile, stand, push the chair in, walk out calmly |

## Body language in a video interview

| Do | Do not |
| --- | --- |
| camera at eye level; face the light | camera looking up your nose; a bright window behind you |
| a tidy, neutral background | a messy room, moving traffic, a bed in view |
| dress as for an in-person interview (at least from the waist up) | casual clothes because "nobody sees" |
| look at the camera for key points | read from a script you can obviously see |
| a nod and a short "I see" to show listening | typing or looking away while the interviewer speaks |
| mute notifications, close other apps | being interrupted by pings |

## Match voice, words and face

When your words say one thing and your body says another, people trust the body. If you say "I am very excited about this role" in a flat voice with arms crossed, they will not believe it. Practise saying important sentences with **the face and voice that match**.

:::tip
Watch a recording of yourself with the **sound off** for a minute: you will see your gestures, posture and habits (swaying, touching your hair). Then listen **without the picture**: you will hear your pace, fillers and tone.
:::
