## Questions are a good sign

Questions mean people were listening. Treat each as a chance to show you understand your topic, not as an attack.

## A simple method for answering

| Step | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| **1. Listen fully** | do not interrupt, and do not prepare your answer while they are still speaking |
| **2. Pause** | take a breath; it shows you are thinking |
| **3. Clarify if needed** | "Do you mean...?" |
| **4. Repeat or rephrase** (in a big room) | so everyone has heard it: "The question is about how we tested the model." |
| **5. Answer briefly and directly** | the answer first, then one reason or example |
| **6. Check** | "Does that answer your question?" |

Keep answers to **about 30 to 60 seconds**. Long, wandering answers lose people and invite more questions.

## When you do not know

Saying "I don't know" honestly is much better than inventing something. Add what you **will** do:

| Weak | Strong |
| --- | --- |
| silence, or a guess presented as a fact | "I do not have the exact figure now. I will check and email you by tomorrow." |
| "Nobody asked me that before." | "That is a good point I have not tested yet. My guess is X, but I would want to check." |
| arguing | "I may be missing something; could you tell me more?" |

For a **viva or interview** question you cannot answer: say what you **do** know that is close, then say what you would do to find out. That shows how you think.

:::example A strong answer to a hard question
**Question:** "Why did you choose logistic regression and not a neural network?"

**Answer:** "With only 400 records, a neural network was likely to overfit, and logistic regression is easy to explain to the college staff who will use it. I did try a small decision tree as well, and logistic regression was just as accurate. With more data I would test a neural network."
:::

The answer gives a decision, reasons and honesty about alternatives.

## Difficult questions and people

| Situation | How to respond |
| --- | --- |
| **A hostile or sarcastic question** | stay calm, do not take it personally; answer the fact inside it: "That is a fair concern about the sample size. Here is what we did..." |
| **A question that is really a speech** | thank them, pick the question inside: "The main question I hear is about cost. Our estimate is..." |
| **A question you already answered** | answer again, kindly and briefly, as if for the first time |
| **A very long, multi-part question** | write it down, "Let me take those one at a time" |
| **A question off topic** | "Great question, and a bit beyond today's talk; let us talk after the session." |
| **A person who keeps interrupting** | "I will come to that in a minute", or "Please hold that until the end, and I will answer it fully." |
| **A mistake pointed out by someone else** | thank them: "You are right, thank you for catching that." Correct it, and continue. |

## Nerves during question time

| Tip | |
| --- | --- |
| prepare **likely questions** in advance | ask a friend: "What would you ask me?" |
| write the two or three hardest ones and rehearse the answers | |
| keep your body language open: do not step back or cross your arms | |
| speak at the same pace as before | |
| remember that you know your project better than anyone in the room | |

## A project viva or panel

A viva is a conversation about **your own work**, so the examiners want to confirm that you **understand and did it**.

| Do | Do not |
| --- | --- |
| know every line, graph and number in your report | include anything you cannot explain |
| explain the **why** behind each decision | memorise answers without understanding |
| bring a printed summary and your laptop with a demo ready | rely on live internet |
| admit limitations honestly | claim a perfect result |
| keep answers short, then pause | talk until they interrupt |
| thank the panel at the end | leave abruptly |

Practise with friends who ask questions **you did not prepare**: "Why this dataset?", "What would you change?", "What was the hardest part?", "What does this number mean?"

## Phrases that help

| Purpose | Phrase |
| --- | --- |
| buy thinking time | "That is an interesting question; let me think for a moment." |
| clarify | "Just to make sure I understand, are you asking about...?" |
| be honest | "I am not sure; here is how I would find out..." |
| disagree politely | "I see it a little differently, and here is why..." |
| move on | "I want to make sure we cover the main points; let us talk about this after." |
| thank | "Thank you for the question." |
