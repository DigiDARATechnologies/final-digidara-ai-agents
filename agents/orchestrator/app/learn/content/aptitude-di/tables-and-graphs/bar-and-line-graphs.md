## What these graphs show

- **Bar graph**: compares values for categories (years, branches) by the height of bars.
- **Line graph**: shows how a value **changes over time**; steeper lines mean faster change.

In a test the picture is given, but the method is the same: **read each value into a small table first**, then calculate.

## Example: bar graph

Students placed by a college each year:

| Year | 2019 | 2020 | 2021 | 2022 | 2023 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Placed | 120 | 90 | 150 | 180 | 210 |

```text
2019 | ############ 120
2020 | #########    90
2021 | ############### 150
2022 | ################## 180
2023 | ##################### 210
```

:::example Increase
By how much did placements increase from 2021 to 2022, and by what percent?

**Solution.** 180 - 150 = **30**, and 30 / 150 x 100 = **20%**.
:::

:::example Highest percentage rise
In which year was the percentage rise over the previous year the highest?

**Solution.**
- 2020: 120 to 90 is a **fall** of 25%.
- 2021: (150 - 90) / 90 = 66.67%.
- 2022: 30 / 150 = 20%.
- 2023: 30 / 180 = 16.67%.

The highest rise is in **2021**.
:::

:::example Average
What was the average number placed per year?

**Solution.** (120 + 90 + 150 + 180 + 210) / 5 = 750 / 5 = **150**.
:::

## Example: line graph with two lines

Profit (in lakh rupees) of two companies:

| Year | 2020 | 2021 | 2022 | 2023 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A | 40 | 50 | 45 | 70 |
| B | 30 | 55 | 60 | 65 |

:::example Comparison
In which year was A's profit more than B's?

**Solution.** 2020: 40 > 30 (yes). 2021: 50 < 55. 2022: 45 < 60. 2023: 70 > 65 (yes). So in **2020 and 2023**.
:::

:::example Growth over the period
What was the percentage growth of A from 2020 to 2023?

**Solution.** (70 - 40) / 40 x 100 = **75%**.
:::

:::example Steepest change
In which interval did B's profit change the most?

**Solution.** Changes: 2020 to 2021: +25; 2021 to 2022: +5; 2022 to 2023: +5. The greatest change is **2020 to 2021** (the steepest part of the line).
:::

## Stacked and grouped bars

- **Grouped bars**: several bars per category side by side, compare them directly.
- **Stacked bars**: one bar per category split into parts; the **total** is the full height. Read each part as the section's length, not its top value.

:::trick
Check the **scale** first: if each gridline is 20, a bar halfway between 80 and 100 is 90, not 85. Misreading the scale costs the most marks.
:::
