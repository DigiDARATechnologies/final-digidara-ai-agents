## Fractions as percentages

| Fraction | Percent | Fraction | Percent |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1/2 | 50% | 1/9 | 11.1% |
| 1/3 | 33.3% | 1/11 | 9.09% |
| 1/4 | 25% | 1/12 | 8.33% |
| 1/5 | 20% | 1/15 | 6.67% |
| 1/6 | 16.67% | 1/16 | 6.25% |
| 1/7 | 14.28% | 1/20 | 5% |
| 1/8 | 12.5% | 1/25 | 4% |

:::example Using the table
Express 17/40 as a percentage.

**Solution.** 1/40 = 2.5%, so 17/40 = 17 x 2.5 = **42.5%**.
:::

## Compare ratios without dividing

To decide which of a/b and c/d is bigger, **cross-multiply**: compare a x d with c x b.

:::example Which is bigger
Which is greater, 7/15 or 9/20?

**Solution.** 7 x 20 = 140 and 9 x 15 = 135. Since 140 is larger, **7/15 is greater**.
:::

This is very useful when a question asks "in which year was the percentage the highest?" You compare the fractions directly and skip the percentages.

## Average by deviation

Assume a convenient value, find each deviation from it, and add the deviations.

:::example
Find the average of 62, 68, 71, 59 and 65.

**Solution.** Assume 65. Deviations: -3, +3, +6, -6, 0. Their sum is 0, so the average is **65**. (The sum 325 divided by 5 is 65.)
:::

## Percentage change shortcuts

- A rise from 80 to 100 is 20/80 = **25%**. But a fall from 100 to 80 is 20/100 = **20%**. The base changes.
- If a value rises by x% and then falls by x%, it does **not** return to the start: it ends lower by x squared / 100 percent.
- Growth of 10% twice is 21% (1.1 x 1.1 = 1.21), not 20%.

## Estimation

When options are far apart, round to simple numbers.

:::example Estimate
About what percent is 487 of 1,990?

**Solution.** 487 is about 500 and 1,990 is about 2,000. 500 / 2000 = **25%** (the exact answer is 24.5%).
:::

## Time-saving habits

1. **Read the question before the data** so you look only at the cells you need.
2. Skip long calculations when the options differ by a lot.
3. Use **ratios** instead of actual numbers when the totals are the same.
4. Keep **units** consistent (crore with crore, thousand with thousand).
5. Do the **easy sets first**; leave a data set that looks long for the end.

:::trick
To find x% of a number that ends in zeros, drop the zeros, take the percent, then put them back. 15% of 2,400: 15% of 24 = 3.6, so 15% of 2,400 = **360**.
:::
