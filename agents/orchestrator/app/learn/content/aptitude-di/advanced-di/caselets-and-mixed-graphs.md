## Caselets

A **caselet** gives the data in a short paragraph. Turn it into a **table or diagram** before answering.

:::example College caselet
A college has 1200 students, of whom 60% are boys. 40% of the boys and 50% of the girls study science. The rest study arts.

**Q1.** How many boys and girls are there?
**Q2.** How many students study science?
**Q3.** What percentage of all students study arts?

**Solution.**

| | Boys | Girls | Total |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Students | 720 | 480 | 1200 |
| Science | 288 | 240 | 528 |
| Arts | 432 | 240 | 672 |

1. Boys = 60% of 1200 = **720**, girls = **480**.
2. Science = 40% of 720 + 50% of 480 = 288 + 240 = **528**.
3. Arts = 1200 - 528 = 672, which is 672 / 1200 = **56%**.
:::

## Caselets with ratios

:::example Ratio caselet
In a company the ratio of men to women is 5 : 3. The total is 640. 20% of the men and 25% of the women are managers. How many managers are there?

**Solution.** Parts = 8, one part = 80. Men = 400, women = 240. Managers = 20% of 400 + 25% of 240 = 80 + 60 = **140**.
:::

## Mixed graphs

Mixed questions give a **table plus a chart**, or two charts, and ask you to combine them.

:::example Table and pie
**Table:** a college has 2000 students in total. **Pie chart** of the students by department: CSE 35%, ECE 25%, Mechanical 20%, Civil 20%.
**Table:** the percentage of students in each department who got placed: CSE 80%, ECE 60%, Mechanical 50%, Civil 40%.

**Q.** How many students were placed in total?

**Solution.** Students per department: CSE 700, ECE 500, Mechanical 400, Civil 400.
Placed: CSE 80% of 700 = 560; ECE 60% of 500 = 300; Mechanical 50% of 400 = 200; Civil 40% of 400 = 160.
Total placed = 560 + 300 + 200 + 160 = **1220**.
:::

:::example Which department
In the same data, which department had the **highest number** of placed students, and which the highest placement **percentage**?

**Solution.** Highest number: **CSE (560)**. Highest percentage: **CSE (80%)**.
:::

## Missing data

Some questions leave a value out; use the totals to find it.

:::example Find the missing value
A bar chart shows sales of 5 months: 40, 55, ?, 60 and 45 (in thousands). The average of the five months is 52. Find the missing value.

**Solution.** Total = 5 x 52 = 260. Known sum = 40 + 55 + 60 + 45 = 200. Missing = **60**.
:::

## Approach

1. Extract all the numbers and write them in a **table**.
2. Fill in the blanks with the totals and percentages.
3. Answer each question from the table.
4. Check that rows and columns add up.

:::remember
Never solve a caselet from memory of the paragraph. Build the table first; every question then becomes a quick look-up.
:::
