## What evaluators look for

| Skill | What it looks like |
| --- | --- |
| **Content** | relevant facts and examples |
| **Communication** | clear, simple, confident speaking |
| **Listening** | referring to others' points |
| **Teamwork** | inviting quieter members, staying polite |
| **Leadership** | guiding the discussion and summarising |
| **Composure** | staying calm when someone disagrees |

The goal is **not** to speak the most. Two or three good points beat a lot of noise.

## Starting a GD

If you know the topic, **start** with a definition, a fact or a short framing of the issue. This gets you noticed. If you are unsure, do not rush; speak after you have a good point.

> "Before we discuss remote work, let me frame it: it has benefits like flexibility and costs like weaker team bonding. Let us look at both."

## The PREP structure for any point

- **P**oint: state your view.
- **R**eason: say why.
- **E**xample: give a fact or an example.
- **P**oint: repeat it briefly.

"I believe online learning is useful (point) because it is flexible and low cost (reason). For example, many students prepare for placements through free courses (example). So it is a strong supplement to classrooms (point)."

## Useful phrases

- Agreeing: "I agree with Ravi, and I would add..."
- Disagreeing: "I see your point, but have we considered..."
- Entering: "May I add a point here?"
- Including others: "Meena, what is your view on this?"
- Redirecting: "That is interesting, but to come back to the main topic..."
- Summarising: "To sum up, we agreed that... and we differed on..."

## What not to do

- Interrupt or talk over people.
- Dominate, shout or get personal.
- Stay silent for the whole discussion.
- Repeat the same point again and again.
- Give false statistics. Say "around" or "I read that" if unsure.
- Use only one-word answers.

## Types of topics

| Type | Examples | Approach |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Current affairs** | digital payments, AI in jobs | facts, effects, balanced view |
| **Social** | social media and youth, women in the workforce | both sides and solutions |
| **Business** | startups vs jobs, e-commerce growth | numbers, trends |
| **Abstract** | "Is zero a number?", "Red or blue?" | creative, structured thinking |
| **Case study** | a company problem to solve | analyse, prioritise, decide |

## Balanced view template for any topic

1. **Definition** and why it matters now.
2. **Advantages**, with an example.
3. **Disadvantages or risks**, with an example.
4. **Way forward**: what individuals, companies or governments should do.
5. **Conclusion**: a one-line balanced stand.

## Ending

If the group is winding down, offer a summary: it shows leadership and clarity, even if you did not speak first.
