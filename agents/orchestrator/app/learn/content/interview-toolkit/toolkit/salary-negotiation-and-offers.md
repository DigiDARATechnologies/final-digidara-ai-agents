## Understand the numbers

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **CTC** (cost to company) | total yearly cost to the company, including benefits |
| **Fixed pay** | guaranteed monthly components (basic, allowances) |
| **Variable pay** | depends on performance, not guaranteed |
| **Deductions** | provident fund (PF), income tax, professional tax, insurance |
| **In-hand (take-home)** | what reaches your bank account each month |
| **Joining bonus** | one-time payment, often with a condition to stay for some time |
| **Notice period** | how long you must work after resigning |

Two offers with the same CTC can have very different in-hand pay depending on the **fixed** share and deductions. Ask for the **salary breakup**.

## Questions to ask about the offer

- How much of the CTC is fixed and how much variable?
- What is the in-hand monthly salary after deductions?
- Is there a **bond or service agreement**? What is its length and penalty?
- What are the **review cycles** and typical increments?
- What does the **training or probation** period look like?
- What is the work location, hybrid or remote policy, and shifts?
- What benefits are included (insurance, learning budget)?

## Negotiating respectfully

For **mass-hiring fresher drives**, packages are often fixed. Negotiation works better when you have:

- a **strong skill or portfolio**,
- **another offer** (honestly),
- or experience.

How to ask:

1. **Thank them** and show enthusiasm: "Thank you, I am excited about this role."
2. **State your reason**: "Based on my skills in X and market research for this role, I was hoping for a package closer to Y."
3. **Ask, do not demand**: "Is there any flexibility?"
4. Be ready to hear "no" and decide calmly.

:::example
"Thank you for the offer, I am very interested in joining. Based on my internship experience and the projects I built, I was expecting something closer to 5 lakh. Is there any flexibility on the package?"
:::

## Beyond salary

If the salary is fixed, you might ask about a **joining bonus**, **faster review**, a **learning budget**, flexible location, or a better role. The first job is also about **learning and the team**.

## Comparing offers

| Factor | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Learning and mentoring | strongest effect on your first five years |
| Role and technology | what you will build and learn |
| Company stability | the risk of layoffs |
| Manager and team | daily experience |
| Growth path | promotions, internal moves |
| Compensation | fixed pay, increments |
| Location and flexibility | cost of living, commute |
| Service agreement | how free you are to move |

## Accepting or declining

- Read the **offer letter** and any agreement completely. Ask questions in writing.
- Reply on time and professionally.
- If you decline: thank them, give a short honest reason and stay polite. You may meet them again.
- Never accept and then not join without telling them; it harms your reputation.

## Avoid scams

A genuine company does **not** ask you to pay for a job, training or a laptop before joining. Verify the company and the email domain. Be careful with offers that arrive without any interview.
