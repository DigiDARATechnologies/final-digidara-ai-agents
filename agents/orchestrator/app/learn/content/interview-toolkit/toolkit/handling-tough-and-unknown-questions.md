## When you do not know the answer

Everyone gets a question they cannot answer. The interviewer is watching **how you react**.

1. **Pause** for a moment and breathe. A few seconds of silence is normal.
2. **Say what you do know**: "I have not used that directly, but I know it is related to X."
3. **Reason aloud**: "I would expect it to work like this, because..."
4. **Ask a clarifying question** if the question is unclear.
5. **Say how you would find out**: "I would check the documentation and test it on a small example."
6. **Move on** gracefully and do not let it affect the next answer.

:::example
Interviewer: "What is a B+ tree?" Candidate: "I know B-trees are used for database indexes to keep data sorted for fast search. I have not studied the B+ tree in detail, but I believe it is a variation where all the data sits in the leaf nodes. I would read about it after this interview."
:::

## Never bluff

A wrong answer given confidently is worse than an honest partial answer. Interviewers can usually tell, and follow-up questions will expose it.

## Weakness questions

Choose a **real** weakness that is not essential for the job, show **what you are doing about it**, and give **proof of progress**.

- Weak: "I am a perfectionist." (everyone says this)
- Good: "I used to avoid public speaking. I joined a speaking club and presented our project to 60 people last semester. I am still improving."

## Gaps in education or career

Explain **briefly and honestly**, then show how you used the time: courses, projects, family responsibility, preparation for exams. Do not be defensive; move on to what you can do now.

## Low marks or backlogs

Accept the fact, explain the reason in one sentence, and show **improvement and skills**: "My first-year marks were low because I struggled to adjust. My marks improved each semester, and I built three projects that show my skills."

## Stress questions

Examples: "Why should we hire you when others have better marks?" "Convince me to buy this pen." They test composure.

- Stay calm and smile.
- Think for a moment, then structure the answer.
- Show confidence without arrogance.

For the pen: understand the needs ("What do you write most?"), highlight 2 benefits, give proof, and ask for the decision.

## Handling a difficult interviewer

If someone is cold, interrupts or challenges you, stay polite, keep eye contact, and answer **the question**. They may be testing your reaction.

## Recover from a mistake

If you realise your answer was wrong, say: "Sorry, let me correct that." Correcting yourself shows honesty and awareness.

## Mindset

- A single weak answer rarely decides the result; the overall impression matters.
- Treat the interview as a conversation, not an exam.
- Prepare, but do not memorise word for word; it sounds robotic.
