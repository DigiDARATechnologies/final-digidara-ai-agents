## Read the job description like a checklist

A job description (JD) tells you what the interview will cover.

1. **Highlight** the required skills (Python, SQL, communication).
2. **Highlight** the duties (build APIs, analyse data, support customers).
3. For each skill, write one **example** from a project or course.
4. Note the **gaps** and decide how you will honestly address them ("I have used SQL for two projects, and I am practising window functions").

| JD says | Prepare |
| --- | --- |
| "Strong in Python" | a Python project and 5 core concept answers |
| "Good communication" | a story where you explained something clearly |
| "Team player" | a group project example with your role |
| "Knowledge of databases" | SQL queries, joins, indexing |
| "Willing to learn" | a recent thing you taught yourself, with proof |

## Research the company (20 to 30 minutes)

- **What they do**: products, services, customers.
- **Size and locations**: how big, where the office is.
- **Recent news**: a launch, funding, award or a new project.
- **Technology**: from the careers page, blog or job posts.
- **Culture and values**: how they describe themselves.
- **Competitors**: a name or two.
- **Your interviewers** on LinkedIn (their roles, not private details).

## Turn research into answers

Instead of "I like your company", say: "I saw that you recently launched a mobile payments feature for small shops. I built a billing app for a local store, so this work interests me."

## Questions to ask the interviewer

Good questions show interest and thinking:

- What does a typical day look like for someone in this role?
- What will the first three months of training or onboarding include?
- Which technologies does the team use, and how are decisions made?
- What does success in this role look like after a year?
- What do you enjoy most about working here?

Avoid asking only about salary, leave or work from home in the first interview.

## One-page prep sheet

Write on one page: company in two lines, the role, your top three strengths with examples, two gaps with your plan, three questions to ask. Read it before you walk in.
