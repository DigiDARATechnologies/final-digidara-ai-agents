## Idioms

An **idiom** is a fixed phrase whose meaning is different from its words.

| Idiom | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A piece of cake | very easy | The exam was a piece of cake. |
| Break the ice | start a conversation in an awkward situation | He told a joke to break the ice. |
| Burn the midnight oil | study or work late at night | She burnt the midnight oil before the exam. |
| Once in a blue moon | very rarely | We meet once in a blue moon. |
| Under the weather | slightly ill | I am under the weather today. |
| Hit the nail on the head | say exactly the right thing | You hit the nail on the head. |
| Cost an arm and a leg | very expensive | That phone costs an arm and a leg. |
| Let the cat out of the bag | reveal a secret | He let the cat out of the bag about the party. |
| Spill the beans | reveal a secret | Don't spill the beans. |
| The ball is in your court | it is your turn to decide | I have made my offer; the ball is in your court. |
| Bite the bullet | face a painful situation bravely | I had to bite the bullet and apologise. |
| On cloud nine | extremely happy | She was on cloud nine after the result. |
| Out of the blue | suddenly, unexpected | He called me out of the blue. |
| Call it a day | stop working | Let's call it a day. |
| Beat around the bush | avoid the main point | Don't beat around the bush; tell me. |
| A blessing in disguise | something bad that turns out good | Losing that job was a blessing in disguise. |
| Add fuel to the fire | make a bad situation worse | His remark added fuel to the fire. |
| Cry over spilt milk | worry about something that cannot be changed | It's done; no use crying over spilt milk. |
| Kill two birds with one stone | achieve two things with one action | By visiting the bank on the way, I killed two birds with one stone. |
| At the eleventh hour | at the last moment | He submitted at the eleventh hour. |

## Phrasal verbs

| Phrasal verb | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **give up** | stop trying, quit | Never give up. |
| **look after** | take care of | She looks after her mother. |
| **look into** | investigate | The police will look into the case. |
| **call off** | cancel | The match was called off due to rain. |
| **put off** | postpone | Don't put off your work. |
| **carry out** | perform, complete | They carried out the plan. |
| **come across** | find by chance | I came across an old photo. |
| **turn down** | refuse | He turned down the offer. |
| **break down** | stop working; lose control | The car broke down. |
| **run out of** | have none left | We ran out of milk. |
| **get along with** | have a good relationship | I get along with my teammates. |
| **take after** | resemble (a family member) | He takes after his father. |
| **bring up** | raise a child; mention | She was brought up in Madurai. |
| **set up** | establish | They set up a new company. |
| **pass away** | die | His grandfather passed away. |
| **go through** | experience; examine | Let's go through the report. |
| **make up** | invent; reconcile | He made up an excuse. |
| **hold on** | wait | Hold on for a minute. |

:::example
"He **turned down** the job offer." The phrasal verb means:
(a) accepted (b) refused (c) reduced (d) delayed

**Solution.** **(b) refused**.
:::

:::example
Choose the best meaning of "to burn the midnight oil".
(a) to waste oil (b) to work late into the night (c) to cook at night (d) to feel sleepy

**Solution.** **(b) to work late into the night**.
:::

:::tip
Learn five idioms and five phrasal verbs a day, each with your own sentence. After 20 days you will know over 200.
:::
