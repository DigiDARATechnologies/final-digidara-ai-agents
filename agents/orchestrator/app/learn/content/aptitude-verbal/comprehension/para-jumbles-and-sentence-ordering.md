## What you do

You get four or five sentences (labelled A, B, C, D) in the wrong order. You must arrange them into a **logical paragraph**.

## Method

1. **Find the opening sentence**: it introduces the subject and does not depend on an earlier sentence. It usually has no pronoun like "this", "he", "they" referring back.
2. **Find the closing sentence**: it often concludes, gives a result or a summary.
3. **Look for linking words**: "however", "therefore", "also", "for example", "as a result" connect to something before them.
4. **Track pronouns**: "he", "it", "this" need a noun before them.
5. **Make mandatory pairs** (two sentences that must stay together) and join them.
6. Check the final order **reads smoothly**.

## Clues

| Clue | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Names or topic appear in full | likely near the **start** |
| "This", "such", "these" | refers to the **previous** idea |
| "However", "but" | contrast with the previous sentence |
| "Therefore", "thus", "so" | result of the previous sentence |
| "First", "then", "finally" | order in time or steps |
| Definition first, then example | "for example" comes after the idea |

:::example
Arrange the sentences:
A. As a result, many people now prefer to shop online.
B. The internet has changed the way people buy things.
C. It allows them to compare prices without leaving home.
D. Online shopping is quick and convenient.

**Solution.** B introduces the topic (the internet changed buying). D gives the example (online shopping is convenient). C explains with "It allows them..." ("It" refers to online shopping in D). A gives the result ("As a result"). Order: **B D C A**.
:::

:::example Process order
A. Then pour hot water over the tea leaves.
B. First, boil some water.
C. Finally, add milk and sugar to taste.
D. Let it stand for two minutes.

**Solution.** "First" gives B. Then A, then D (waiting), then "Finally" C. Order: **B A D C**.
:::

## Sentence ordering with a given first line

Sometimes the first sentence is fixed (marked S1). Use it as the topic and find which sentence naturally follows it by meaning and pronouns.

:::trick
If two options start differently, test just the first sentence: usually only one or two candidates make a good opening. That cuts the choices quickly.
:::
