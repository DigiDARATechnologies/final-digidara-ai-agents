## The strategy

1. **Skim the questions first** (not the options) to know what to look for.
2. **Read the passage once**, quickly, noting the main idea of each paragraph.
3. For each question, go back and find the **line that proves** the answer.
4. **Eliminate** wrong options, then choose the best remaining one.

Aim for about 5 to 6 minutes per passage of 250 to 400 words.

## Question types

| Type | What it asks | How to answer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Main idea / title** | what the passage is mainly about | cover the whole passage, not a single detail |
| **Detail** | something stated directly | find the exact lines |
| **Inference** | what is suggested, not stated | choose what **must** be true from the text |
| **Vocabulary in context** | meaning of a word as used | test each option in the sentence |
| **Tone / attitude** | the writer's feeling | look at word choice (critical, neutral, optimistic) |
| **Purpose** | why a detail is included | what job does it do (example, contrast, proof) |

## A short example

:::example Passage and questions
"Many students believe that studying late at night helps them learn more. However, research shows that sleep is vital for memory. During sleep, the brain organises what was learnt during the day. Students who sleep well before an exam usually remember more than those who stay awake revising."

**Q1. What is the main idea?**
(a) Students should avoid exams. (b) Sleep helps memory, so late-night study is not always useful. (c) The brain is active at night. (d) Revision is harmful.

**Solution.** The passage contrasts late study with the value of sleep for memory: **(b)**.

**Q2. The word "vital" means:**
(a) optional (b) harmful (c) essential (d) tiring

**Solution.** "Sleep is vital for memory" means it is necessary: **(c) essential**.

**Q3. Which can be inferred?**
(a) Sleep has no effect on exam results. (b) Students who sleep well may perform better in exams. (c) All late-night study is useless. (d) Memory works only during sleep.

**Solution.** The passage says well-rested students "usually remember more", so **(b)**. (c) and (d) are too extreme.
:::

## Removing wrong options

Wrong options usually fall into these groups:

- **Too extreme**: contain "always", "never", "only", "all" when the passage is moderate.
- **Out of scope**: true in real life but not in the passage.
- **Opposite**: the reverse of what is said.
- **Half right**: starts correct and ends wrong.

## Tips

- Read **news editorials and articles** daily; speed comes from practice.
- Do not carry **your own opinions** into the answer.
- For tone questions, words like "unfortunately", "disappointing" show a negative tone; "promising", "welcome" a positive one.

:::remember
The answer is always in the passage. Find the line, then choose the option that matches it.
:::
