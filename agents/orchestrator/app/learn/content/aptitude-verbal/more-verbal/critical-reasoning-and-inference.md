- To weaken an argument, break the link; to strengthen it, support the link

## Parts of an argument

- **Conclusion**: the point the author wants you to accept.
- **Premises**: the reasons given.
- **Assumption**: something unstated that must be true for the reasons to prove the conclusion.

:::example
"Our app's rating has risen from 3.5 to 4.5 since we added a dark mode. So dark mode made users happier."

- Conclusion: dark mode made users happier.
- Premise: the rating rose after the dark mode was added.
- Assumption: nothing else caused the rise in rating.
:::

## Question types

| Question | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| **Find the assumption** | find the missing link; negate it and see if the argument fails |
| **Strengthen** | pick the option that supports the missing link |
| **Weaken** | pick the option that breaks the link (offers another cause, a counter-example) |
| **Inference** | pick what **must** be true from the passage |
| **Flaw** | find the logical mistake (such as confusing correlation with cause) |
| **Conclusion** | pick what the passage most logically leads to |

## Weaken and strengthen

:::example Weaken
"Students who attend coaching classes score higher marks. Therefore coaching classes improve marks." Which statement weakens this?
(a) Coaching fees are high.
(b) Students who are already top performers are the ones who choose to join coaching.
(c) Some students do not attend any coaching.
(d) Coaching centres have good teachers.

**Solution.** **(b)** gives another explanation: the higher marks may be because of who joins, not because of coaching.
:::

:::example Strengthen
Which statement strengthens the same argument?
(a) Students who joined coaching improved their own marks compared with their earlier results.
(b) Coaching is expensive.
(c) Most students join coaching in cities.

**Solution.** **(a)** shows the improvement within the same students, supporting the cause.
:::

## Inference

An **inference** is something that must be true based on the passage, not merely possible.

:::example
"All employees who completed the training were promoted. Ravi was promoted."

Which can be inferred?
(a) Ravi completed the training. (b) Ravi may have completed the training. (c) Ravi did not complete the training.

**Solution.** Being promoted does not prove he completed the training (others may also be promoted), so **(b)** is the safe statement. (a) does not necessarily follow.
:::

## Common flaws

- **Correlation is not causation**: two things happening together does not mean one causes the other.
- **Hasty generalisation**: using too few examples.
- **Appeal to authority**: "A famous person said so."
- **False choice**: pretending there are only two options.

:::remember
Always ask "What has to be true for this to work?" That is the assumption, and attacking or supporting it answers most critical reasoning questions.
:::
