takeaways:
- Every word has a job: noun, pronoun, verb, adjective, adverb, preposition, conjunction, interjection
- Tense shows time (past, present, future) and aspect (simple, continuous, perfect)
- Keep tenses consistent inside a sentence unless the time really changes

## The eight parts of speech

| Part | Job | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Noun** | names a person, place, thing or idea | **Asha** bought a **book**. |
| **Pronoun** | replaces a noun | **She** gave it to **him**. |
| **Verb** | action or state | She **reads**. He **is** tired. |
| **Adjective** | describes a noun | a **tall** boy |
| **Adverb** | describes a verb, adjective or adverb | He ran **quickly**. |
| **Preposition** | shows relation to a noun | **on** the table, **in** May |
| **Conjunction** | joins words or clauses | tea **and** coffee, I stayed **because** it rained |
| **Interjection** | sudden feeling | **Wow!** **Oh!** |

The same word can have different jobs: "Light **work**" (noun) and "I **work** daily" (verb).

## The twelve tenses

| Tense | Simple | Continuous | Perfect | Perfect continuous |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Present** | I write | I am writing | I have written | I have been writing |
| **Past** | I wrote | I was writing | I had written | I had been writing |
| **Future** | I will write | I will be writing | I will have written | I will have been writing |

## When to use them

- **Simple present**: habits and facts. "The sun rises in the east."
- **Present continuous**: happening now. "She is reading."
- **Present perfect**: past action with a link to now, **no definite time**. "I have finished my work."
- **Simple past**: finished action at a definite time. "I finished it yesterday."
- **Past perfect**: the earlier of two past actions. "The train **had left** when I reached the station."
- **Future**: "I will call you tomorrow."

## Frequent tense mistakes

| Wrong | Right | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I have seen him yesterday. | I **saw** him yesterday. | a definite past time needs the simple past |
| He is knowing the answer. | He **knows** the answer. | state verbs (know, like, want) do not take -ing |
| If it will rain, we will stay home. | If it **rains**, we will stay home. | no future tense after "if" in the condition |
| She said that she will come. | She said that she **would** come. | shift the tense in reported speech |
| I am living here since 2020. | I **have been living** here since 2020. | "since" and "for" need perfect tenses |

:::trick
"Since" gives a **starting point** (since 2020, since Monday). "For" gives a **period** (for five years). Both use the **present perfect**, not the simple present.
:::

:::example Choose the right option
By the time we reached the cinema, the film ____.
(a) started (b) had started (c) has started (d) starts

**Solution.** The film began before we reached, so the earlier action uses the past perfect: **(b) had started**.
:::
