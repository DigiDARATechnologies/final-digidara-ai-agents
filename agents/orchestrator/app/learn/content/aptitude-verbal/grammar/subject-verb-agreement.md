---
title: Subject-verb agreement
summary: Make the verb match its subject in number, including tricky cases like 'each', 'neither', collective nouns and 'there is'.
takeaways:
- A singular subject takes a singular verb, a plural subject a plural verb
- Ignore words between the subject and verb when finding the real subject
- Each, every, either, neither and one take singular verbs
---
## The basic rule

- Singular subject: **The boy plays.**
- Plural subject: **The boys play.**

## Cases that confuse

### 1. Words that come between

Find the **real subject**; ignore phrases such as "along with", "as well as", "together with", "in addition to".

- The **teacher**, along with the students, **is** coming. (subject: teacher)
- The **price** of the books **has** gone up.

### 2. Each, every, either, neither, one, anybody, everyone

These are singular.

- **Each** of the girls **has** a pen.
- **Neither** of the answers **is** correct.
- **Everyone** **was** present.

### 3. Either...or / neither...nor

The verb agrees with the **nearer** subject.

- Neither the teacher nor the **students** **are** ready.
- Either the students or the **teacher** **is** responsible.

### 4. Both...and: always plural

- **Both** Ravi and Asha **are** here.

### 5. Collective nouns

Words like team, committee, class, family are singular when the group acts as **one**, plural when members act **separately**.

- The committee **has** decided. (as a unit)
- The committee **are** divided in their views. (members differ; less common)

### 6. Uncountable and "plural-looking" nouns

- News, mathematics, physics, economics, politics: **singular**. "The news **is** good."
- Scissors, trousers, glasses, clothes: plural. "My trousers **are** new."
- "A pair of shoes **is**" (the subject is "pair").

### 7. There is / there are

The verb agrees with what comes **after**.

- There **is** a book on the table. There **are** two books on the table.

### 8. Quantities and money

- Fifty rupees **is** a small amount. Ten kilometres **is** a long walk.
- "A number of students **are** absent" (plural), but "**The** number of students **is** 40" (singular).

:::example Find the error
"The number of applicants for the post are very high."

**Solution.** "The number" is singular, so the verb must be **is**. Correct: "The number of applicants for the post **is** very high."
:::

:::example Fill the blank
Neither the manager nor the employees ____ aware of the change.
(a) was (b) is (c) were (d) has been

**Solution.** The nearer subject is "employees" (plural): **(c) were**.
:::

:::remember
Find the true subject, decide singular or plural, then match the verb. Ignore the words in between.
:::
