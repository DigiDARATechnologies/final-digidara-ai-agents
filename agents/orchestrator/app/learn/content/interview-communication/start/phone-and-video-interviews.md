## Why online interviews are different

Many first rounds are on the phone, on video, or on a platform that records your answers. You lose some natural signals (handshake, shared space) and gain risks: bad internet, echo, interruptions. Success depends on **preparation and set-up**.

## The set-up checklist

| Item | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| **Internet** | test your speed; use a cable or sit near the router; have a phone hotspot ready |
| **Device** | laptop on a stable surface, **fully charged** and plugged in; close other apps |
| **Camera** | at eye level (stack books under the laptop); face a window or lamp so that light is on your face, not behind you |
| **Microphone** | a headset or earphones with a mic usually sound better than a laptop mic |
| **Sound** | quiet room; tell family; turn off fans, TV and phone notifications |
| **Background** | tidy and neutral; avoid moving objects, posters or a bed in view |
| **Software** | install and test the app (Zoom, Teams, Google Meet) and update it the day before |
| **Name and photo** | a professional display name (your real name) and no joke profile picture |
| **Documents** | resume, notes, pen and paper, a glass of water, within reach but not visible on camera |
| **Join** | 5 to 10 minutes early; check the link and time zone |

Dress as you would for an in-person interview. It affects your mindset as well.

## On camera

| Do | Do not |
| --- | --- |
| look at the **camera** when you speak, and at the screen when listening | stare at your own image or look away constantly |
| sit upright, a little forward, hands visible and relaxed | slouch, lean back, hide your hands, fidget |
| smile at the beginning and when appropriate | keep a frozen face |
| nod and use short reactions ("I see") to show you are listening | stay completely still |
| wait a half-second after they finish before answering (lag causes overlap) | interrupt because of the delay |
| speak a little slower and clearer than normal | mumble or rush |

If you read notes, keep **keywords**, not full sentences, near the camera, and avoid obvious reading.

## Phone interviews

You cannot be seen, so your **voice** carries everything.

| Tip | Why |
| --- | --- |
| stand up or sit upright | your voice sounds more energetic |
| smile while speaking | it changes your tone, and people hear it |
| go to a quiet place with good signal; charge the phone | avoids drops |
| keep your resume and notes in front of you | confidence |
| speak slowly; pause at the end of ideas | clarity |
| answer in short, structured points | easy to follow without visual cues |
| say your name when picking up: "Hello, this is Priya Nair." | professional |

## Recorded video interviews and online tests

Some companies ask you to record answers (one-way video interviews) or to take timed online tests with a camera on.

| Tip | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| **Read the instructions** | how many tries, how much time, can you re-record |
| **Practise** | record yourself first on your phone |
| **Plan your answers** | use the first 15 to 30 seconds of thinking time, outline in your head: point, example, close |
| **Look at the camera** | not at the screen preview |
| **Be energetic and clear** | a recorded answer without energy seems flat |
| **Online tests** | keep your ID ready; do not open other tabs; do not use unfair methods: proctoring tools detect them, and cheating ends your application |

## When technology fails

| Problem | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| **Internet drops** | rejoin quickly; message or email the interviewer; offer to switch to a phone call; stay calm |
| **They cannot hear you** | check the mic setting; unmute; try a headset; type in the chat while fixing it |
| **Background noise** | apologise briefly; mute when not speaking; move to a quieter place |
| **Frozen screen** | say "I think my connection froze. Could you repeat the last part?" |
| **Interrupted by someone at home** | apologise briefly, continue; interviewers understand |

Interviewers know technology fails. They care more about **how you handle it** than the glitch itself.

## Group and panel interviews online

| Tip | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| mute when not speaking | reduces noise |
| use the "raise hand" feature or wait for a pause | avoids talking over others |
| speak to everyone, not only the person who asked | include the whole panel |
| use names when responding | "Thank you, Mr Das." |
| keep the camera on if allowed | helps connection |

## Phone and email manners with recruiters

| Situation | Do |
| --- | --- |
| a recruiter calls | answer politely, or if you cannot talk, say "Thank you for calling. I am not able to speak right now. Could we talk at 4 pm?" |
| a missed call from an unknown number | call back politely: it may be an interview |
| a voicemail | speak clearly and give your name and number twice |
| a message on WhatsApp | reply politely, within working hours |
| your caller tune and voicemail greeting | keep them professional while job hunting |

:::example Reply to an interview invitation
**Subject:** Re: Interview invitation: Python Developer

Dear Ms Kapoor,

Thank you for inviting me. I confirm my availability on Thursday, 12 March at 11:00 am for the video interview. I will join using the link you sent. Please let me know if I should bring or prepare anything.

Regards,
Priya Nair
:::

:::tip
Do a **full test call** with a friend the day before: same room, same device, same app. Record it and watch it. You will notice problems with light, sound and posture that you cannot see yourself.
:::

:::practice communication-coach
Record a two-minute answer on video and review it for eye contact, pace and clarity using the checklist above.
:::
