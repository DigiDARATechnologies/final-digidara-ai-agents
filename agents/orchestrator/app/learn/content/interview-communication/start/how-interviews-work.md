## The usual stages

| Stage | What happens | How to prepare |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Application and resume screening** | software and recruiters scan resumes | tailored one-page resume with matching keywords |
| **Online test** (aptitude, coding, English) | timed questions | practise the aptitude and coding sections of this Hub |
| **Group discussion** (some companies) | a topic is discussed by a group while observers watch | the group discussion lesson |
| **Technical interview** | questions on skills, projects and problem solving | your projects, the technical and interview Q&A sections |
| **Managerial interview** | scenarios, teamwork, how you work | STAR stories (next lessons) |
| **HR interview** | background, motivation, salary, fit | self-introduction, common HR answers |
| **Offer and background check** | documents, references | honest, consistent information |

Campus drives often compress these into one or two days; off-campus hiring can take weeks.

## What interviewers are really judging

Behind every question, the interviewer is asking some version of three things:

| They ask themselves | They look for |
| --- | --- |
| **Can you do the job?** | skills, projects, problem solving, learning ability |
| **Will you do the job?** | motivation, interest in the role, reliability, honesty |
| **Will you work well with us?** | communication, teamwork, attitude, how you handle pressure and feedback |

Fresher interviewers know you do not have years of experience. They look for **potential**: curiosity, fundamentals, clear thinking, and attitude.

## Prepare in five steps

**1. Research the company**

| Find out | Where |
| --- | --- |
| what they do, their products, customers | the website, "About" page |
| recent news | news search, LinkedIn page |
| their values and culture | careers page, social media |
| the role | the job description: list the **skills** and **responsibilities** |
| people you may meet | LinkedIn (professional, not stalking) |

**2. Match yourself to the role.** Read the job description and write three columns: *What they want*, *What I have*, *My example*. Each requirement should have a proof from a project, internship or activity.

**3. Prepare your stories.** Have 5 or 6 short stories ready (STAR method, next lesson): a project you are proud of, a team situation, a problem you solved, a failure and what you learned, a time you led, a time you learned something quickly.

**4. Prepare your introduction and answers** to the usual questions (later lessons).

**5. Prepare your questions** for them (a later lesson).

## Practical preparation

| Item | Checklist |
| --- | --- |
| **Documents** | several printed copies of your resume, ID proof, mark sheets, certificates, passport photos, a notebook and pen |
| **Clothes** | clean, ironed, simple and formal; shoes polished; check the dress code |
| **Journey** | know the route; plan to arrive **15 minutes early** |
| **Phone** | silent or off, and out of sight |
| **Health** | sleep well the night before; eat something light; drink water |
| **Online interview** | test camera, microphone, internet and the software the day before; use a quiet room; keep a charger and a backup phone for a hotspot |

## First impressions

People form an impression in the first minute, and it influences the rest of the interview.

| Do | Do not |
| --- | --- |
| greet everyone politely, including the receptionist and the security guard | treat staff rudely: it is often reported to the interviewers |
| greet with a smile and, if offered, a firm handshake | arrive late, flustered or on the phone |
| wait to be asked to sit; sit upright | slouch, cross your arms, tap your feet |
| speak clearly, make eye contact | whisper, mumble or avoid looking at anyone |
| keep a calm, positive tone | complain about your college, your previous employer or your teachers |

## During the interview

| Tip | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| **Listen to the whole question** | answer the question asked, not the one you prepared |
| **Take a breath before answering** | a short pause is fine |
| **Structure your answers** | point, reason, example |
| **Be honest** | if you do not know, say so and describe how you would find out |
| **Show enthusiasm** | interest in the work counts as much as skill |
| **Give examples** | stories beat claims |
| **Watch the time** | 1 to 2 minutes per answer, unless a longer story is asked for |
| **Do not interrupt** | even if you know the answer |

## After the interview

1. Send a short, polite **thank-you email** the same day or the next morning.
2. Make notes of the questions you were asked and how you answered. They are gold for the next interview.
3. Wait for the date they gave; if none, follow up politely after 5 to 7 working days.
4. Whatever the result, ask for feedback if appropriate, and keep improving.

:::example Thank-you email
**Subject:** Thank you: Python Developer interview

Dear Mr Rao,

Thank you for the opportunity to interview for the Python Developer position today. I enjoyed learning about your team's work on the billing platform. Our discussion made me even more interested in the role.

Please let me know if I can provide anything further.

Regards,
Priya Nair
:::
