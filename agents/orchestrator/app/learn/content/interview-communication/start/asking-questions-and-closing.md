## "Do you have any questions for us?"

Never say "No, nothing". It sounds uninterested. Prepare **three to five** thoughtful questions and ask two or three, depending on time.

| Good questions | Why they work |
| --- | --- |
| "What would a successful first six months look like in this role?" | shows you care about performance |
| "What does a typical day or week look like for someone in this position?" | shows practical interest |
| "What training or mentoring do new joiners get?" | shows you want to learn |
| "What are the biggest challenges the team is facing right now?" | shows you think about solving problems |
| "How is performance measured and feedback given?" | shows maturity |
| "What do you enjoy most about working here?" | builds rapport |
| "What are the next steps in the process, and when can I expect to hear back?" | practical close |

## Questions to avoid in early interviews

| Avoid | Why | Better |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "What does your company do?" | you should know | ask about something specific you read |
| "How much leave will I get?" or "When will I get a raise?" (as the first question) | sounds as if you only care about perks | save practical details for HR or after the offer |
| "Can I work from home all the time?" | sounds like you avoid work | ask about the work-style policy neutrally |
| anything you could find on their website | shows poor research | ask for opinions and experiences |
| questions that criticise ("Why is your Glassdoor rating low?") | sounds aggressive | "What is the culture like?" |

## Closing the interview

The last minute matters. Close with:

1. **Thank them** for their time.
2. **Restate interest** and a reason: "After hearing about the project, I am even more excited about this role."
3. **Summarise your fit** in one sentence, if there is a natural moment.
4. Ask about **next steps** (if not already discussed).
5. A **smile, a handshake** (if appropriate), and a polite exit.

:::example Strong close
"Thank you for your time. Hearing about your onboarding mentoring made me even more interested in joining the team. I believe my Python skills and project experience would help me contribute quickly. Could you tell me what the next steps are?"
:::

## The offer

When you receive an offer:

| Step | Action |
| --- | --- |
| **Thank them** | warmly and immediately |
| **Read it carefully** | role, pay (CTC breakdown), joining date, location, notice, bond or service agreement, probation |
| **Ask for time if needed** | "Thank you. May I review it and respond by Friday?" |
| **Ask questions** | about anything unclear (variable pay, bond, benefits, relocation) |
| **Reply in writing** | accept or decline politely by email |

### Understanding the pay

In India, offers are quoted as **CTC** (cost to company), which includes the pay you receive and company contributions. Ask for a **breakup**:

| Part | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Fixed pay** | monthly salary components (basic, HRA, allowances) |
| **Variable / bonus** | paid only if targets are met; ask if it is guaranteed |
| **Employer contributions** | provident fund, gratuity, insurance (part of CTC but not in your monthly hand) |
| **Take-home** | what reaches your bank each month after tax and deductions |
| **Bond / service agreement** | a period you must stay or repay money; read it carefully |

:::warning
Beware of any "recruiter" or company that asks you to **pay money** for a job, a training fee, a security deposit or a laptop before joining. Genuine employers do not charge you to hire you. Verify the company independently and do not share bank or identity details until the offer is confirmed through official channels.
:::

## Negotiating politely

For many first jobs, the pay is fixed, but it is acceptable to ask politely, especially if you have a competing offer or strong skills.

| Do | Do not |
| --- | --- |
| research typical pay | demand a number with no reason |
| show gratitude and interest first | threaten to leave |
| give reasons: skills, certifications, other offers, market data | compare yourself to a friend's salary |
| negotiate the **whole package** (joining bonus, learning budget, remote days, review timeline) if pay is fixed | focus only on the number |
| accept the final answer gracefully | argue or get emotional |

:::example Negotiation script
"Thank you very much for the offer; I am excited about the role. Based on my research and my skills in Python and SQL, I was hoping for something closer to ₹X. Is there any flexibility in the package?"
:::

## Declining politely

Keep the door open: you may meet these people again.

:::example Declining an offer
**Subject:** Re: Offer for Software Engineer

Dear Ms Rao,

Thank you very much for offering me the Software Engineer position. I truly appreciate the time you and the team spent with me. After careful consideration, I have decided to accept another offer that fits my current career goals. I hope our paths cross again, and I wish you and the team all the best.

Regards,
Arun
:::

## Handling rejection

Rejection is part of every career, including those of very successful people.

| Do | Do not |
| --- | --- |
| thank them and ask for feedback politely | argue or send an angry message |
| review: what went well, what to improve | blame the interviewer |
| practise the weak areas: technical gaps, communication, nerves | give up after two or three attempts |
| keep applying; many people get an offer after 10 to 30 interviews | measure your worth by one result |

:::tip
After every interview, write down the questions you were asked and what you would answer differently. In ten interviews you will have a personal study guide. That is how people improve fast.
:::
