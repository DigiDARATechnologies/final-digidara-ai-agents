---
title: Strengths, weaknesses and tricky HR questions
summary: Answer questions about strengths, weaknesses, failures, salary expectations, leaving a job and other tricky topics with honesty and confidence.
takeaways:
- Be honest, specific and positive: show self-awareness, not perfection
- Support strengths with examples; show a real weakness and what you are doing about it
- Do not criticise anyone, and do not lie
---
## "What are your strengths?"

Pick **two or three** strengths relevant to the role and give a **proof** for each.

| Weak | Strong |
| --- | --- |
| "I am hard-working, sincere and punctual." | "I am a quick learner. In three weeks I taught myself Flask, and used it for my project." |
| "I am a team player." | "I like helping teammates. In our hackathon I helped two juniors fix their code, and our team still submitted early." |

Formula: **strength + example + what it means for the employer.**

Useful strengths for freshers: learning quickly, problem solving, attention to detail, communication, teamwork, reliability, curiosity, adaptability.

## "What is your weakness?"

The interviewer is checking **self-awareness** and your willingness to improve.

| Do | Do not |
| --- | --- |
| choose a **real** but **not essential-to-the-job** weakness | say "I have no weakness" |
| show what you are **doing** to improve | give a fake weakness such as "I work too hard" or "I am a perfectionist" |
| be brief and calm | confess a serious problem ("I get angry at teammates") |

:::example Honest answer
"I used to find public speaking difficult, and I would avoid presenting. In my third year I decided to work on it: I joined the debate club and now volunteer for the first slot in every project review. I am much more comfortable now, though I still prepare carefully."
:::

Structure: **the weakness, what it caused, what you did, the progress.**

Examples of reasonable weaknesses for freshers: limited work experience (and how you are gaining it), nervousness in public speaking, difficulty saying no, spending too long on details, not asking for help early. Pick one that is true for you.

## "Tell me about a failure"

Use STAR, choose something real, own it, and finish with what you learned. Do not choose a failure that shows poor integrity.

## "What is your expected salary?"

For freshers, many companies have a fixed pay for the role, so the question may be only a check.

| Approach | Say |
| --- | --- |
| **Research first** | look up typical pay for that role, company size and city (job sites, college seniors) |
| **Give a range, not a single number** | "Based on my research, I expect between X and Y, but I am flexible depending on the role and learning opportunities." |
| **Ask about their range** | "What is the pay range for this role?" |
| **Stay flexible** | for a first job, learning and growth often matter more than a small difference in pay |

Avoid naming a very high number that ends the conversation, or an extremely low one that undervalues you.

## "Why are you leaving your current job?" (for experienced candidates)

| Do | Do not |
| --- | --- |
| say what you are **moving towards** ("I want more responsibility, new technology") | speak badly about your boss, colleagues or company |
| stay positive and brief | complain or share office conflict |
| be consistent with your resume | lie about the reasons |

:::example Answer
"I learned a lot in my first two years, but my work has become routine. I am looking for a role with more challenging projects, and this position fits that."
:::

## "Do you have other offers?" and "Will you join if we select you?"

Be honest and professional. If you have other interviews, say you are exploring options, but that this role is of strong interest. If you have an offer with a deadline, you can mention it politely. Never lie about offers.

## "How do you handle pressure and criticism?"

Use a real example. Show that you stay calm, ask for clarification, and treat feedback as useful:

:::example Answer
"When my project guide said my first report was unclear, I was disappointed, but I asked for specific examples. I rewrote the report using a clear structure, and he said the second version was much better. I now ask for feedback early."
:::

## Personal and sensitive questions

Interviewers should not ask about religion, caste, marital status, family plans, politics or health in a hiring decision. If such questions are asked:

| Option | Example |
| --- | --- |
| answer briefly and move on, if you are comfortable | "I am happy to relocate; family will not be a problem." |
| redirect to the job | "I can assure you that I am committed to this role and my work schedule is flexible." |
| politely decline | "I would prefer to focus on my skills and fit for the role." |

You have the right to be treated with respect. If a company repeatedly asks inappropriate questions, that tells you something about the workplace.

## Questions to prepare answers for

| Category | Questions |
| --- | --- |
| About you | strengths, weaknesses, hobbies, biggest achievement, proudest project |
| Motivation | why this job, why this company, goals, what motivates you |
| Teamwork | disagreement, helping someone, working with a difficult person, leadership |
| Problem solving | a challenge you solved, a time you learned something quickly |
| Pressure | deadlines, mistakes, feedback, criticism |
| Practical | salary, notice period, relocation, shifts, gaps, other offers |

## Golden rules for every answer

1. **Be honest.** Interviewers check; lies are discovered.
2. **Be specific.** Examples beat adjectives.
3. **Be positive.** Do not criticise anyone.
4. **Be brief.** 1 to 2 minutes.
5. **Be yourself.** Prepared, not scripted.
