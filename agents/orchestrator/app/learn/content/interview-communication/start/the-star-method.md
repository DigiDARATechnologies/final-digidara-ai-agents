## Behavioural questions

Interviewers believe that **how you behaved before** predicts how you will behave in future. So they ask:

- "Tell me about a time you worked in a team."
- "Describe a situation where you faced a difficult deadline."
- "Tell me about a mistake you made."
- "Give an example of a time you showed leadership."
- "Tell me about a conflict with a teammate."

Generic answers ("I am a good team player") are weak. They want a **specific story**.

## The STAR method

| Letter | Meaning | What to say | Share of the story |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **S** | **Situation** | where and when, in one or two sentences | 15% |
| **T** | **Task** | your responsibility or the challenge | 10% |
| **A** | **Action** | what **you** did, step by step (use "I") | 55% |
| **R** | **Result** | the outcome, with numbers if possible, and what you learned | 20% |

Most of your time should be on **Action** and **Result**: that is what the interviewer wants to hear.

## Worked example: a team conflict

**Question:** "Tell me about a time you disagreed with a teammate."

:::example STAR answer
**Situation:** "In my final-year project, our team of four had to choose a database. A teammate wanted MongoDB, while I preferred MySQL."

**Task:** "We had only two weeks to build the prototype, and we could not afford to waste time arguing. I wanted to settle it fairly."

**Action:** "I suggested we list what our project actually needed: relationships between students, courses and marks, and simple reports. We scored both options against these needs together. I also asked him to explain his reasons, and I found out that he was worried about handling changing data fields. We agreed to use MySQL, and I added a flexible JSON column for the changing fields to address his concern."

**Result:** "We finished the prototype on time, and our faculty praised the design. I learned that conflict is easier to resolve when we compare ideas against shared goals, and listen to the real worry behind a suggestion."
:::

Why it works: specific, "I" statements, a clear process, a positive result and a lesson.

## Worked example: a mistake

**Question:** "Tell me about a mistake you made."

:::example STAR answer
**Situation:** "During my internship, I was asked to update a spreadsheet of 500 customer records."

**Task:** "I was responsible for the final accuracy."

**Action:** "I used a find-and-replace formula without checking a sample first. It changed phone numbers in some rows by mistake. I noticed it when a colleague called the wrong number. I told my supervisor at once, restored the file from the backup, and redid the work with a check on ten sample rows before applying changes to everyone."

**Result:** "The error was fixed within an hour and no customer was affected. Since then I always keep a backup and test on a small sample first, and I now use that method in all my projects."
:::

Choose a **real but small-to-moderate** mistake, take responsibility (do not blame others), show how you fixed it and what you changed. Never claim "I have never made a mistake".

## Worked example: a deadline

:::example STAR answer
**Situation:** "Two days before our department's technical fest, the registration page crashed."

**Task:** "As the web coordinator, I needed to have it working before registrations opened."

**Action:** "I checked the error logs and found that the form was failing when more than 50 people submitted at once. I changed the code to queue the submissions and added a limit to the form. I tested with 200 sample entries, and asked two friends to try it on their phones."

**Result:** "The page handled 480 registrations over the next two days without an error, and the organisers asked me to build the page for next year too."
:::

## Build your story bank

Prepare **five or six stories** and note which questions each one can answer:

| Story | Can answer questions about |
| --- | --- |
| A project you are proud of | achievement, technical skill, problem solving, initiative |
| A team project | teamwork, communication, conflict, leadership |
| A mistake or failure | learning, honesty, resilience |
| A tight deadline or pressure | time management, stress, prioritising |
| Learning something new quickly | adaptability, curiosity |
| Helping or leading someone | leadership, empathy, responsibility |

Sources for stories: academic projects, internships, college clubs and events, sports, part-time jobs, volunteering, hackathons, helping family or neighbours with something practical.

## Tips

| Do | Do not |
| --- | --- |
| use **"I"**, even in team stories: say what you personally did | say only "we", which hides your contribution |
| be specific: names (without private data), numbers, dates | speak in generalities |
| finish with the **result and the lesson** | stop in the middle of the story |
| keep each answer to about **2 minutes** | ramble |
| practise each story aloud until it feels natural | memorise it word for word |
| pick a **positive** ending, even for failure stories (what you learned) | blame others |

:::remember
Quick STAR check after every story: Did I say **what I did**? Did I give a **result**? Could the interviewer picture the situation?
:::
