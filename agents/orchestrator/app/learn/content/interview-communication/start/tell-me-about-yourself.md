## "Tell me about yourself"

This is a request for a **short professional story**, not your life history. Use the **Present, Past, Future** structure from the introduction lesson and tailor it to the job.

:::example Fresher answer for a software role
"I am Meena Raj, a final-year B.Tech Information Technology student. I enjoy building things with code. In my third year I built a Flask web app for our department that lets students book lab slots; about 120 students use it every week. During my internship at a startup I wrote Python scripts that automated a daily report. I am now looking for a software developer role where I can learn from experienced engineers and work on real products, which is why this position interests me."
:::

| Do | Do not |
| --- | --- |
| 30 to 90 seconds | talk for five minutes |
| focus on skills and results that fit the role | start with your family background |
| end by linking to this role | stop without a link to the job |
| sound natural | recite a memorised paragraph |

## "Why do you want to work here?"

The interviewer wants to see **interest and research**. Generic answers such as "It is a reputed company" are forgettable.

| Weak | Strong |
| --- | --- |
| "It is a big and reputed company." | "I read that you are building tools for rural healthcare. I did my project on patient records, so this work interests me." |
| "I need a job." | "I want to start my career in data, and your analytics team works on retail problems which I find exciting." |
| "My friend works here." | "A senior from my college works here and told me that new engineers get to work on live projects from the first month." |

Formula: **something specific about the company + how your skills or interests connect + what you would like to learn or contribute.**

## "Why do you want this role?"

Show you understand the work:

:::example Answer
"I enjoy analysing data and explaining what it means. In my project, I found that three cities accounted for 18% of orders, and presenting that to my faculty was the part I liked most. A data analyst role lets me do both."
:::

## "What do you know about our company?"

Prepare **four facts**: what the company does, one or two products or clients, something recent (news, expansion, award), and its values or culture.

:::example Answer
"You provide cloud accounting software to small businesses. I read that you recently launched a mobile app for GST filing, which seems useful for small shops like my uncle's. I also saw that you value learning, with a mentoring programme for new graduates."
:::

If you truly know very little, be honest, but never say you know nothing: it signals you did not care enough to prepare.

## "Why should we hire you?"

A good answer has three parts: **what you bring, proof, and how it helps them.**

:::example Answer
"I think I would be a strong fit for three reasons. I have solid Python and SQL skills, which I have used in two projects. I learn quickly: I taught myself Flask in three weeks to finish my project. And I work well in teams: I led a group of six for our hackathon entry. I believe I can start contributing quickly and keep growing with your team."
:::

Do not compare yourself to other candidates or criticise them.

## "Where do you see yourself in five years?"

They want to know you are **ambitious but realistic and committed**.

:::example Answer
"In five years I hope to have grown into a confident, well-rounded engineer with strong skills, and maybe taking on responsibility such as leading a small module. I want to keep learning, and I see this company as a place where I can do that."
:::

| Avoid | |
| --- | --- |
| "I will be your manager" or "I will start my own company" (sounds like you will leave) | |
| "I have no idea" | |
| an unrelated plan ("I want to study abroad") unless true and you are comfortable | |

## "Are you willing to relocate or work in shifts?"

Be honest. If you are flexible, say so. If you have a real limit, explain it politely and briefly.

:::example Answer
"Yes, I am open to relocating. I am excited to learn in a new city."
:::

## "Do you have any gap in your studies?"

Answer **calmly and honestly**, then show what you did: preparation for exams, health reasons, a course, family responsibility. Do not apologise repeatedly; end with what you learned or are doing now.

## A one-page preparation table

| Question | Your key points (write them) |
| --- | --- |
| Tell me about yourself | present, past, future |
| Why this company? | two specific facts plus your link |
| Why this role? | the part of the work you like, with an example |
| Why should we hire you? | three strengths with proof |
| Five-year goal | growth, learning, commitment |
| Relocation, shifts, gap | your honest answers |

Practise saying them aloud. Write **bullet points**, not full sentences, so the words stay natural.
