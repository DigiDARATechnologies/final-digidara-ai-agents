### Q [Easy] Tell me about yourself.
Use Present, Past, Future. "I am Priya Nair, a final-year B.Sc. Statistics student. In my last project I analysed 20,000 sales records using Python and SQL and found that three cities produced 18% of orders. I am looking for a junior data analyst role where I can apply these skills and keep learning." Keep it to about 30 to 60 seconds and link it to the role.

### Q [Easy] Why do you want to join our company?
Name something specific you researched, connect it to your skills or interests, and say what you want to learn. "You build tools for rural healthcare, and my project was about patient record management, so I would like to work on problems that matter to real people."

### Q [Easy] What are your strengths?
Two or three, with proof. "I learn quickly: I taught myself Flask in three weeks to complete my project. I am also good at explaining technical ideas simply, which helped my team during our final presentation."

### Q [Medium] What is your weakness?
A real, non-critical weakness plus action. "I used to avoid public speaking. I joined the debate club and now I volunteer to present first in project reviews. I am much better, but I still prepare carefully."

### Q [Easy] Where do you see yourself in five years?
Ambitious but committed. "I hope to become a confident, skilled engineer who is trusted with responsibility, perhaps leading a small module. I see this company as a place to grow into that."

### Q [Medium] Why should we hire you?
Skills, proof, benefit. "I have solid Python and SQL skills from two projects, I learn fast, and I work well in a team: I led a group of six in a hackathon. I think I can start contributing quickly."

### Q [Medium] Tell me about a time you worked in a team.
Use STAR. Describe the goal, your role, what **you** did, the result and what you learned. Mention how you handled differences.

### Q [Medium] Tell me about a time you faced a conflict.
STAR with a calm, respectful approach: listen, find the shared goal, compromise, and a positive result. Do not blame the other person.

### Q [Medium] Tell me about a mistake or failure.
Pick a real but small to moderate mistake, own it, explain the fix and what you do differently now. "I applied a bulk change without testing on a sample, and corrupted some records. I told my supervisor immediately, restored the backup and now always test on ten rows first."

### Q [Medium] How do you handle pressure or tight deadlines?
Give a real example. "Before our project submission, we found a bug two days earlier. I listed priorities, fixed the critical bug first, asked a teammate to test, and we submitted on time."

### Q [Medium] Describe a time you showed leadership.
Leadership does not require a title. "When our group lacked direction for a hackathon, I proposed splitting tasks by strength, held a 10-minute daily check-in and helped a teammate who was stuck. We finished early."

### Q [Medium] What motivates you?
Be genuine. "I enjoy solving problems and seeing something I built used by real people. When 120 students started using my booking app, that was the best feeling."

### Q [Medium] How do you handle criticism?
"I listen without defending, ask for specific examples, and act on it. My guide said my first report was unclear. I asked what exactly was confusing, rewrote it, and he said the second version was much better."

### Q [Medium] What do you do if you do not know something at work?
"I try to find out myself first by reading documentation or searching; if I am still stuck after a reasonable time, I ask a teammate, with a clear explanation of what I have already tried. And I note the answer so I do not ask twice."

### Q [Medium] How do you prioritise your work?
"I list tasks, check deadlines and impact, and do the urgent and important ones first. I also tell my manager if I cannot meet something so that expectations are clear."

### Q [Medium] Are you willing to relocate or work in shifts?
Be honest. If yes: "Yes, I am open to relocating, and I am excited to learn in a new place." If limited: explain the limit briefly and what you can offer.

### Q [Hard] What is your expected salary?
Research, then give a range and stay flexible. "Based on my research for this role, I expect between X and Y, but I am flexible; the learning opportunity is important to me."

### Q [Medium] Do you have any questions for us?
Always ask two. "What would a successful first six months look like?" and "What training do new joiners receive?"

### Q [Medium] Why is there a gap in your studies or career?
Be honest, brief and positive, then move forward. "I took a year to prepare for competitive exams, and during that time I also completed an online Python course. I am now focused on starting my career."
