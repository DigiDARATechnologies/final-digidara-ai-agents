## The STAR method

When an interviewer says "tell me about a time when...", they want a **real story**, not an opinion. STAR keeps it short and clear:

| Letter | What to say |
| --- | --- |
| **S**ituation | the background, in one or two sentences |
| **T**ask | what you were responsible for |
| **A**ction | what **you** did, step by step |
| **R**esult | what happened, ideally with a number |

Aim for about two minutes. Keep most of the time on Action and Result.

### Q [Medium] Tell me about a time you worked in a team.
*Situation:* In our final-year project, four of us had to build a booking app in six weeks. *Task:* I handled the database and API. *Action:* I agreed a shared document of API formats with the front-end teammate, and we met twice a week. *Result:* We finished two days early and scored the highest marks in the batch.

Use "I" for your own actions, and "we" for the team's outcome.

### Q [Medium] Tell me about a conflict with a teammate.
Pick a small, real disagreement, not a big fight. Show that you **listened first**, found the real issue, and agreed a way forward. End with what you learned. Never blame the other person.

### Q [Medium] Describe a time you failed.
Choose a real but modest failure. Own it ("I missed it because I did not test early"), then spend most of your answer on what you changed and how the next time went better.

### Q [Medium] Tell me about working under pressure or a tight deadline.
Describe the deadline, how you prioritised (what you did first and what you dropped), and the result. Mention that you told people early when something was at risk.

### Q [Medium] Tell me about a time you showed leadership.
Leadership is not always a title. It can be organising your project group, helping a classmate who was stuck, or starting a coding club. Show what you decided and who benefited.

### Q [Medium] Tell me about a time you learned something quickly.
Pick a skill you picked up for a real need. Say how you learned it (documentation, a course, asking someone) and what you built with it. This shows you can keep learning on the job.

### Q [Hard] How do you handle criticism?
Say you listen without defending, ask for a specific example, and change something. Then give a short real case: "My lead said my code was hard to read. I started naming things clearly and adding short comments, and the next review had no comments on style."

### Q [Hard] Tell me about a time you went beyond what was asked.
Choose a case where you saw a problem nobody asked you to fix, and fixed it, such as writing instructions for a lab, or automating a repeated step. Say the result for other people, not just for you.

:::interview
**Prepare five stories**, not fifty answers: a team project, a difficulty, a failure, a time you led, and a time you learned fast. Most behavioural questions can be answered with one of these.
:::

:::practice mock-interview
Practise your STAR stories in a Mock Interview and compare what you said with what you meant.
:::
