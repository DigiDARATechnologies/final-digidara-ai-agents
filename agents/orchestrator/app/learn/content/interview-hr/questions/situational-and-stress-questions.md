---
title: Situational and stress questions
summary: How to answer 'what would you do if' questions, conflicts, pressure, mistakes and tough questions with the STAR method.
kind: qa
takeaways:
- Use STAR: Situation, Task, Action, Result
- Stay calm, be honest and show what you learned
- Choose real examples from projects, college or part-time work
---
Use **STAR**: describe the **S**ituation, your **T**ask, the **A**ction you took and the **R**esult. For "what would you do" questions, explain your **steps** and your **reasoning**.

### Q [Medium] Tell me about a conflict with a teammate and how you handled it.
**Sample.** "In our final-year project, my teammate and I disagreed on the database design (Situation). I needed us to agree before the deadline (Task). I listened to his reasons, we listed the pros and cons of each on paper and tested both with sample data (Action). His design was simpler for our scale, so we chose it, and we finished two days early (Result)." Show respect, listening and a fair method.

### Q [Medium] Tell me about a time you failed. What did you learn?
Choose a real, modest failure and focus on the **lesson**. "I underestimated the time for a coding assignment and submitted it half-finished. Since then I break work into small tasks, add buffer time and start early. My next two projects were submitted on time."

### Q [Medium] What do you do when you are under pressure or have tight deadlines?
"I list tasks by priority, break the big one into small steps, communicate early if something is at risk, and focus on one thing at a time. For example, before our exam week I made a daily plan and finished both the project and my revision."

### Q [Medium] What if you disagree with your manager?
"I would first make sure I understand their reasoning, then share my view politely with facts or data and suggest an alternative. If they still decide differently, I follow the decision and do my best, unless it is unsafe or unethical."

### Q [Medium] How do you handle criticism?
"I listen without defending, ask for a specific example, thank the person and act on it. Feedback on my code reviews has made my code cleaner."

### Q [Medium] What would you do if you could not finish a task on time?
"Tell the team **early**, not at the deadline. Explain the reason, say what is done, propose a new timeline or reduced scope, and ask for help if needed."

### Q [Medium] What would you do if you are asked a question you do not know in a technical interview?
"I would say honestly that I am not sure, then explain how I would approach it or what related concepts I know, and mention how I would find the answer. Guessing confidently is worse than showing a good thought process."

### Q [Medium] Describe a time you showed leadership.
Leadership is not only a title. "I organised our college hackathon team of four: I split the work by strengths, set daily check-ins, and handled the final demo. We finished in the top five."

### Q [Hard] What would you do if a colleague was doing something unethical?
"I would first talk to the colleague privately in case there was a misunderstanding. If it continued or was serious, I would report it through the proper channel (a manager or HR), because honesty and the company's trust matter more than avoiding discomfort."

### Q [Medium] Can you work under a manager who gives you very little guidance?
"Yes. I would clarify the goal, make a plan, and ask short focused questions when stuck. I like taking ownership and sharing progress regularly so the manager can step in if needed."

### Q [Hard] Stress question: "Why should we not hire you?"
Stay calm. Mention a **real but manageable** gap and your plan: "I have less industry experience than some candidates, but I have built three projects, I learn quickly, and I am used to finishing what I start."

### Q [Hard] Stress question: "You seem nervous. Are you?"
"A little, because this opportunity matters to me. I have prepared well, and I think it will settle once we get into the discussion." Stay honest and composed.

:::practice mock-interview
Practise these answers aloud in a Mock Interview. Keep each STAR answer to about 90 seconds.
:::
