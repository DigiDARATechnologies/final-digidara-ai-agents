- Name the company and the role in your own words, not a script

HR rounds usually start with these. They are not trick questions: the interviewer wants to hear whether you can speak clearly about yourself. Use the sample answers as a shape, then replace the facts with your own.

### Q [Easy] Tell me about yourself.
Use three parts, about a minute in total: **who you are now**, **what you have done**, **why you are here**.

*Sample:* "I am a final-year B.E. Computer Science student at XYZ College. Over the last two years I built a student attendance app in Python and Flask, and I completed an internship where I fixed bugs in a React project. I enjoy turning problems into working software, and I am looking for a role where I can keep learning while contributing to a team."

Do not recite your résumé from the beginning, and do not start with your childhood.

### Q [Easy] Why should we hire you?
Match what **they need** with what **you offer**, with one proof point.

*Sample:* "You need someone who can learn quickly and write clean code. In my college project I picked up Flask in two weeks and delivered a working demo on time. I am willing to keep learning and I work well in a team."

Avoid "I am hard-working" with nothing behind it.

### Q [Easy] Why do you want to join our company?
Show you did a little research. Mention one specific thing: a product, a technology, the way they train freshers, their reputation for something.

*Sample:* "I read that your team works on [product]. I am interested in [area], and I like that freshers get structured training and real projects early."

A generic answer such as "it is a big company" tells them nothing.

### Q [Easy] What do you know about our company?
Know the basics: what they do, their main products or services, their size or locations, and one recent piece of news. Spend ten minutes on their website and LinkedIn page the night before.

### Q [Easy] What are your hobbies?
Choose real ones and say what they teach you. "I play cricket for my college team, which taught me to coordinate with others under pressure" works better than a list.

### Q [Medium] Tell me about your family background.
Keep it short, positive and factual: where your family lives, what your parents do, and one line about how they support your education. This is mostly to put you at ease.

### Q [Medium] Why did you choose this course or career?
Tell a short story: when you first felt interested, what you did about it, and what you want to do with it. A real moment is more convincing than "it has good scope".

### Q [Easy] Do you have any questions for us?
Always say yes. Good ones: "What does the first six months look like for a fresher?", "How is the training structured?", "What would success look like in this role?". Do not ask about salary or leave in the first round.

:::interview
**Tip:** record yourself answering "Tell me about yourself" on your phone. Listen once. You will hear filler words and long pauses you did not notice.
:::
