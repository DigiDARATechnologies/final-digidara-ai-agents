- For freshers, be flexible and focus on learning, while staying honest
- Always read the offer letter and service agreement carefully

### Q [Easy] Why do you want to join our company?
Be specific. "I read that your team builds learning platforms using Python and AI, which matches my projects. I like that freshers get structured training and real project work, and I want to grow here." Mention the product, culture or technology, not just the brand name.

### Q [Medium] What do you know about our company?
Know the business, the main products or services, the size, recent news and values. Spend 20 minutes on the website, LinkedIn page and one recent news article before the interview.

### Q [Medium] What are your salary expectations?
For freshers: "I am open to the standard package for this role and I am more interested in learning and growth. I would be happy with what your company offers to freshers with my skills." If pressed, give a **reasonable range** based on research and your market value.

### Q [Medium] Are you willing to relocate or work in shifts?
Answer honestly. "Yes, I am open to relocating." If you have a real limit, state it politely rather than saying yes and refusing later.

### Q [Medium] What is your notice period or when can you join?
Freshers: "I can join as per the company's schedule after my final results and documents." Experienced: state your real notice period and any flexibility.

### Q [Medium] Do you have any other offers?
Be honest and calm. "I have attended a couple of processes, and I am in discussion with one company. Your company is my strong preference because of the role and the learning." Never lie about offers.

### Q [Medium] What do you expect from this job?
"A chance to work on real projects, learn from experienced engineers, get regular feedback and take more responsibility over time."

### Q [Medium] Are you comfortable with a service agreement or bond?
Read the terms carefully (duration, amount, conditions) **before** you sign. You may say "I would like to understand the agreement fully before I decide". Ask HR to explain anything unclear.

### Q [Medium] How do you handle relocation, long hours or a change in role?
"I understand business needs can change. I am flexible, as long as it helps me learn and I am treated fairly."

### Q [Medium] Why did you choose this career or domain?
Connect a real moment to your interest. "I built a small web app for my class timetable and enjoyed seeing people use it. That made me want a career building software."

### Q [Medium] Do you have any questions for us?
Always ask two or three thoughtful questions: "What does the training for new joiners look like?" "What would success in the first six months look like?" "What technologies does the team use?" Do not ask about salary or leave first.

### Q [Hard] How do you decide between two offers?
"I compare learning opportunities, the quality of the team and manager, the role and technology, growth path, company stability, location and, finally, compensation. For a first job I weigh learning most."

### Q [Hard] What do you do if the offer is lower than you expected?
Thank them, ask whether the **fixed** and **variable** parts are clear, and ask about growth, reviews and training. You may politely ask whether there is flexibility, with a reason (a strong skill or another offer), but accept that for fresher drives packages are often fixed.
