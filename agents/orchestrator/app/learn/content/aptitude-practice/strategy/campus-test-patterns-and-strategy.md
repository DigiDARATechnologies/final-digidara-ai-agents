## What a typical test looks like

Companies differ and change their format every year, so **always read the official notice** for the exact pattern. Most campus tests contain some of these sections:

| Section | What it tests | Where to study here |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Quantitative aptitude** | arithmetic, algebra, geometry, data | Quantitative Aptitude course |
| **Logical reasoning** | patterns, puzzles, arrangements | Logical Reasoning course |
| **Verbal ability** | grammar, vocabulary, reading | Verbal Ability course |
| **Data interpretation** | tables and charts | Data Interpretation course |
| **Coding / technical** | programming and basic concepts | Python, DSA, SQL courses |
| **Essay or email writing** | communication | Communication courses |

## Common features

- A **time limit per section** (you often cannot go back to a finished section).
- **Negative marking** in many tests.
- A **cutoff** per section as well as overall, so do not ignore a weak section.
- Online tests with an on-screen calculator in some cases, and only rough paper in others.

## A good time plan

Use a rule such as "about 1 minute per easy question". For a 30 question, 30 minute section:

1. First pass (about 15 minutes): solve everything you can do in under a minute.
2. Second pass (about 10 minutes): attempt medium questions.
3. Last 5 minutes: guess intelligently where negative marking is small, or skip where it is large.

## Accuracy vs speed

- A wrong answer with negative marking can cancel a right answer. If you can remove **two options**, a guess is usually worth it. If you know nothing, skip.
- Do not get stuck: leave a question after about 2 minutes.

## A 4-week preparation plan

| Week | Focus |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Percentages, ratio, averages, profit and loss, interest; learn squares, cubes and fractions to percentages |
| 2 | Time and work, speed, mixtures, number system; logical series, coding-decoding, blood relations |
| 3 | Arrangements, directions, syllogisms; grammar and vocabulary; data interpretation |
| 4 | Full-length timed tests, then analyse every mistake |

## Daily routine

- 30 minutes of new topics, 30 minutes of timed practice, 15 minutes of mistake notes, 10 minutes of vocabulary.

## Test day

- Sleep well, reach early, and carry the required documents and ID.
- Read each question fully; many errors come from missing a word like "not" or "except".
- Keep a steady pace; do not rush the first questions or panic if a section feels hard, since it is hard for everyone.
- Check the **units** (minutes or hours) and the **question asked** (asked for the value, not the percent).

## Mistake notebook

Write down every error with the reason: concept gap, calculation slip, misread question, or time pressure. Review it weekly. This habit improves scores faster than solving new questions.
