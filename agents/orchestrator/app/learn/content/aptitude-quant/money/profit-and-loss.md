---
title: Profit and loss
summary: Cost price, selling price, profit and loss percentage, discounts and marked price.
takeaways:
- Profit and loss percentages are always taken on the cost price
- Discount is taken on the marked price
- Two items sold at the same price, one at a profit and one at a loss of the same percent, always give a loss
---
## The terms

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **CP** | cost price: what the seller paid |
| **SP** | selling price: what the buyer pays |
| **MP** | marked price: the price on the tag |
| Profit | SP − CP (when SP is higher) |
| Loss | CP − SP (when CP is higher) |

:::formula
Profit % = Profit / CP × 100

Loss % = Loss / CP × 100

SP = CP × (100 + Profit %) / 100

CP = SP × 100 / (100 + Profit %)
:::

:::example Finding the percentage
A shirt bought for ₹500 is sold for ₹600. What is the profit percent?

**Solution.** Profit = 100. Profit % = 100 / 500 × 100 = **20%**.
:::

:::example Finding the cost
An article sold for ₹840 gives a 20% profit. What was the cost price?

**Solution.** CP = 840 × 100 / 120 = **₹700**.
:::

## Discount

Discount is on the **marked price**:

:::formula
SP = MP × (100 − Discount %) / 100
:::

:::example Discount and profit
A ₹1000 item is sold at 10% discount. If it cost ₹750, what is the profit percent?

**Solution.** SP = 1000 × 0.9 = 900. Profit = 900 − 750 = 150. Profit % = 150 / 750 × 100 = **20%**.
:::

:::example Choosing the marked price
A trader buys at ₹800 and wants a 25% profit after giving a 20% discount. What price should he mark?

**Solution.** Needed SP = 800 × 1.25 = 1000. MP × 0.8 = 1000, so MP = **₹1250**.
:::

## Successive discounts

Discounts multiply, they do not add.

:::example Two discounts
A shop gives 20% off and then a further 10% off. What is the single equivalent discount?

**Solution.** 0.80 × 0.90 = 0.72, so you pay 72%. The discount is **28%**, not 30%.
:::

## A classic

:::example Same price, a gain and a loss
Two articles are sold at the same price. One gives a 20% profit and the other a 20% loss. What is the overall result?

**Solution.** There is always a loss of (x² / 100)% = 20² / 100 = **4% loss**.
:::

:::remember
Profit and loss use **cost price** as the base. Discount uses **marked price** as the base. Mixing these up is the most common mistake.
:::
