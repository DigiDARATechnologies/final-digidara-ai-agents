---
title: Simple and compound interest
summary: Interest on a sum at a rate for some years, simply and compounded, and the difference between the two.
takeaways:
- Simple interest is the same every year; compound interest earns interest on interest
- A = P(1 + R/100)^T for compound interest
- For 2 years, CI minus SI is P(R/100)^2
---
## Simple interest

Interest is charged only on the original sum, the **principal**.

:::formula
SI = P × R × T / 100

Amount = P + SI
:::

P is the principal, R the rate percent per year, T the time in years.

:::example Simple interest
Find the simple interest on ₹5000 at 8% per year for 3 years.

**Solution.** 5000 × 8 × 3 / 100 = **₹1200**.
:::

## Compound interest

Each year's interest is added to the principal, so next year's interest is larger.

:::formula
Amount = P × (1 + R/100)^T

CI = Amount − P
:::

:::example Compound interest
Find the compound interest on ₹10,000 at 10% per year for 2 years.

**Solution.** Amount = 10,000 × 1.1 × 1.1 = 12,100. CI = 12,100 − 10,000 = **₹2,100**. (Simple interest would have been ₹2,000.)
:::

## The difference for 2 years

:::formula
CI − SI (2 years) = P × (R/100)²
:::

For the example above: 10,000 × (0.10)² = **₹100**. ✓

## Compounding more often

For half-yearly compounding, halve the rate and double the number of periods.

:::example Half-yearly
Find the amount on ₹8000 at 5% per year, compounded half-yearly, for 1 year.

**Solution.** Rate per half-year = 2.5%, periods = 2. Amount = 8000 × 1.025 × 1.025 = 8000 × 1.050625 = **₹8405**.
:::

## Doubling time

| Rule | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Simple interest | money doubles in 100 / R years (at 5%: 20 years) |
| Compound interest (rule of 72) | money doubles in about 72 / R years (at 8%: about 9 years) |

:::trick
When T is a whole number of years, multiply by (1 + R/100) once per year. For small T it is quicker than using a power on paper.
:::

:::remember
Always check the wording: "per annum" means per year, and time given in months must be divided by 12.
:::
