title: Mixtures and alligation
summary: Mix two items to reach a target price or strength using the alligation rule, and handle replacement problems.
takeaways:
- Alligation gives the ratio of the cheaper to the dearer quantity
- Cheaper : dearer = (dearer - mean) : (mean - cheaper)
- Repeated replacement multiplies the pure part by (1 - removed/total) each time
---
## The alligation rule

To mix a cheaper item (price c) and a dearer item (price d) to get a mean price m:

:::formula
Quantity of cheaper : Quantity of dearer = (d - m) : (m - c)
:::

:::example Mixing rice
Rice at 40 per kg is mixed with rice at 60 per kg to get a mixture worth 48 per kg. In what ratio?

**Solution.** Cheaper : dearer = (60 - 48) : (48 - 40) = 12 : 8 = **3 : 2**.
:::

## Milk and water

:::example Adding water
A 60 litre mixture has milk and water in the ratio 2 : 1. How much water must be added to make the ratio 4 : 3?

**Solution.** Milk = 40 L, water = 20 L. Milk stays 40. For 4 : 3 water must be 30 L, so add **10 litres**.
:::

:::example Concentration
How many litres of water must be added to 20 litres of 40% alcohol solution to make it 25% alcohol?

**Solution.** Alcohol = 8 L (it does not change). New total = 8 / 0.25 = 32 L, so add 32 - 20 = **12 litres**.
:::

## Replacement

When you take out part of a pure liquid and replace it with water, repeatedly:

:::formula
Pure liquid left = Initial x (1 - removed / total) to the power n
:::

:::example Two replacements
A container has 40 litres of pure milk. 4 litres are removed and replaced by water. This is done a second time. How much milk is left?

**Solution.** 40 x (1 - 4/40) squared = 40 x (0.9) squared = 40 x 0.81 = **32.4 litres**.
:::

## Profit by mixing

:::example
Tea at 100 per kg and tea at 140 per kg are mixed in the ratio 3 : 2. The mixture is sold at 132 per kg. What is the profit percent?

**Solution.** Cost of 5 kg = 3 x 100 + 2 x 140 = 580, so 116 per kg. Profit = 132 - 116 = 16, which is 16/116 x 100 = about **13.8%**.
:::

:::trick
Draw the alligation cross: put c and d on the left, m in the middle; subtract diagonally to get the ratio.
:::
