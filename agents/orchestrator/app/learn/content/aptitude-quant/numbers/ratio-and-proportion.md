## Sharing in a ratio

A ratio compares quantities. To share a total in a ratio, add the parts and find the value of one part.

:::example Dividing a sum
Divide ₹600 in the ratio 2 : 3 : 5.

**Solution.** Parts = 2 + 3 + 5 = 10. One part = 600 / 10 = 60. Shares are 2 × 60 = **₹120**, 3 × 60 = **₹180**, 5 × 60 = **₹300**.
:::

:::example Ages in a ratio
The ages of two brothers are in the ratio 3 : 5 and add up to 40. What will the ratio be after 5 years?

**Solution.** Parts = 8, one part = 5, so the ages are 15 and 25. After 5 years: 20 and 30, a ratio of 20 : 30 = **2 : 3**.
:::

## Combining ratios

If a : b = 2 : 3 and b : c = 4 : 5, make the common term the same. Multiply the first by 4 and the second by 3:

a : b = 8 : 12 and b : c = 12 : 15, so **a : b : c = 8 : 12 : 15**.

## Direct proportion

Two quantities rise and fall together.

:::example Cost of pens
4 pens cost ₹60. What do 7 pens cost?

**Solution.** One pen = 60 / 4 = ₹15. Seven pens = 15 × 7 = **₹105**.
:::

## Inverse proportion

When one goes up the other comes down, and their **product** stays the same.

:::example Workers and days
6 workers finish a job in 10 days. How long will 15 workers take?

**Solution.** Total work = 6 × 10 = 60 worker-days. 60 / 15 = **4 days**.
:::

| Situation | Type |
| --- | --- |
| more items, more cost | direct |
| more workers, fewer days | inverse |
| more speed, less time for the same distance | inverse |

## Alligation: mixing

When two items at different prices are mixed to get a target price:

:::formula
Cheaper quantity : Dearer quantity = (Dearer − Mean) : (Mean − Cheaper)
:::

:::example Mixing rice
Rice at ₹30/kg is mixed with rice at ₹45/kg so the mixture costs ₹36/kg. In what ratio?

**Solution.** Cheaper : Dearer = (45 − 36) : (36 − 30) = 9 : 6 = **3 : 2**.
:::

:::trick
Check by weighted average: 3 kg at ₹30 and 2 kg at ₹45 cost 90 + 90 = ₹180 for 5 kg, which is ₹36/kg. ✓
:::
