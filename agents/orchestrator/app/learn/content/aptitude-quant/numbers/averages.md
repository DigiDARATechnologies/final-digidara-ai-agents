## The idea

:::formula
Average = Sum of values / Number of values

So **Sum = Average × Number of values**.
:::

Almost every average problem is solved by turning the average into a **sum**, changing the sum, and turning it back.

:::example Adding a number
The average of 5 numbers is 20. A sixth number is added and the average becomes 22. What is the new number?

**Solution.** Old sum = 5 × 20 = 100. New sum = 6 × 22 = 132. New number = 132 − 100 = **32**.
:::

:::example Weighted average
In a class, 30 students average 60 marks and 20 students average 70. What is the average of all 50?

**Solution.** Total = 30 × 60 + 20 × 70 = 1800 + 1400 = 3200. Average = 3200 / 50 = **64**. (Not 65: more students scored lower.)
:::

:::example Replacing a person
The average age of 8 people is 40. One person aged 32 leaves and a new person joins, and the average becomes 42. How old is the new person?

**Solution.** The sum rose by 8 × 2 = 16, so the newcomer is 16 years older than the person who left: 32 + 16 = **48**.
:::

## Useful results

| Fact | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Sum of the first n natural numbers | n(n + 1) / 2 |
| Average of the first n natural numbers | (n + 1) / 2 |
| Average of the first 10 numbers | 5.5 (sum 55) |
| Average of consecutive numbers | the middle one |

## Average speed

For the **same distance** at two speeds x and y:

:::formula
Average speed = 2xy / (x + y)
:::

:::example Going and coming back
A car goes at 40 km/h and returns the same way at 60 km/h. What is the average speed?

**Solution.** 2 × 40 × 60 / (40 + 60) = 4800 / 100 = **48 km/h**.
:::

:::remember
Average speed is **total distance divided by total time**, never the simple average of the speeds (that would give 50).
:::
