## Angles

:::formula
Sum of angles of a triangle = 180 degrees
Exterior angle of a triangle = sum of the two opposite interior angles
Sum of interior angles of a polygon with n sides = (n - 2) x 180 degrees
Each interior angle of a regular polygon = (n - 2) x 180 / n
:::

:::example Polygon
Find the sum of the interior angles of a hexagon and each angle of a regular octagon.

**Solution.** Hexagon: (6 - 2) x 180 = **720 degrees**. Regular octagon: (8 - 2) x 180 / 8 = **135 degrees**.
:::

Angles on a straight line add to 180 degrees; angles around a point add to 360 degrees. In a quadrilateral they add to 360 degrees.

## Right triangles and Pythagoras

:::formula
hypotenuse squared = base squared + height squared
Common triples: (3, 4, 5), (5, 12, 13), (8, 15, 17), (7, 24, 25) and their multiples
:::

:::example Ladder
A 13 m ladder leans against a wall and reaches 12 m up. How far is its foot from the wall?

**Solution.** 13 squared - 12 squared = 169 - 144 = 25, so the distance is **5 m**.
:::

## Similar triangles

Triangles with the same angles are **similar**: their sides are in the same ratio.

:::formula
If the sides are in the ratio a : b, then the **areas** are in the ratio a squared : b squared.
:::

:::example Areas
Two similar triangles have corresponding sides 3 cm and 5 cm. Find the ratio of their areas.

**Solution.** 3 squared : 5 squared = **9 : 25**.
:::

:::example Shadows
A 6 m pole casts a 4 m shadow. At the same time a tree casts a 20 m shadow. How tall is the tree?

**Solution.** Heights are proportional to shadows: 6 / 4 = h / 20, so h = **30 m**.
:::

## Circles

- The angle at the **centre** is **twice** the angle at the circumference on the same arc.
- The angle in a **semicircle** is 90 degrees.
- A tangent is perpendicular to the radius at the point of contact.
- If two chords cross, the product of the parts of one chord equals the product of the parts of the other.

:::example Angle at the centre
An arc subtends an angle of 40 degrees at the circumference. What angle does it subtend at the centre?

**Solution.** **80 degrees**.
:::

## Area of a triangle

- Half x base x height.
- For an **equilateral triangle** with side a, area = (sqrt(3) / 4) x a squared.

:::trick
A right triangle with sides in the ratio 3 : 4 : 5 is so common that spotting it saves a whole square-root calculation.
:::
