## Problems on numbers

:::example Sum and difference
The sum of two numbers is 40 and their difference is 6. Find the numbers.

**Solution.** Larger = (40 + 6) / 2 = 23 and smaller = (40 - 6) / 2 = **17**.
:::

:::example Digit problem
A two-digit number has a tens digit twice its units digit. If 27 is subtracted, the digits are reversed. Find the number.

**Solution.** Let the units digit be u and the tens digit 2u. The number is 10(2u) + u = 21u and the reversed number is 10u + 2u = 12u. Then 21u - 12u = 27, so 9u = 27 and u = 3. The number is **63**.
:::

:::example Fractions
A number is increased by 20% and then decreased by 20%. The result is 96. Find the original number.

**Solution.** The net factor is 1.2 x 0.8 = 0.96. Original x 0.96 = 96, so the original is **100**.
:::

## The chain rule

If one quantity changes, work out the effect on the other.

### Direct proportion

:::example Pens
5 pens cost 40 rupees. What do 8 pens cost?

**Solution.** One pen costs 8 rupees. 8 pens cost **64 rupees**.
:::

### Inverse proportion

More workers means **fewer** days.

:::example Men and days
12 men build a wall in 15 days. How many days will 20 men take?

**Solution.** Total work = 12 x 15 = 180 man-days. Days = 180 / 20 = **9 days**.
:::

### Many quantities together

:::formula
Work is proportional to (men) x (days) x (hours per day) x (efficiency)
:::

:::example Machines
6 machines make 360 items in 4 hours. How many items will 8 machines make in 5 hours?

**Solution.** Items per machine per hour = 360 / (6 x 4) = 15. Then 8 x 5 x 15 = **600 items**.
:::

:::example Food stock
A camp has food for 200 people for 30 days. After 10 days, 50 people leave. For how many more days will the food last?

**Solution.** After 10 days, food left for 200 people for 20 days = 4,000 person-days. With 150 people: 4,000 / 150 = **26.67 days** (about 26 days and 16 hours).
:::

## Efficiency

If A is twice as efficient as B, A takes half the time. If their efficiency ratio is 3 : 2, their time ratio for the same work is 2 : 3.

:::trick
Decide first whether the relation is **direct** (same direction) or **inverse** (opposite). Multiply across for direct, cross-multiply for inverse.
:::
