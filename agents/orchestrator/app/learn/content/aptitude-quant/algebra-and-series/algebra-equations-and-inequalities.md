## Linear equations

Do the same thing to both sides until the unknown is alone.

:::example
Solve 3x + 7 = 22.

**Solution.** 3x = 15, so x = **5**.
:::

## Two equations, two unknowns

Add or subtract the equations to remove one unknown.

:::example Simultaneous
x + y = 10 and x - y = 2. Find x and y.

**Solution.** Add: 2x = 12, so x = 6. Then y = 4. Answer: **x = 6, y = 4**.
:::

:::example Word form
The sum of two numbers is 25 and their difference is 7. Find the numbers.

**Solution.** Larger = (25 + 7) / 2 = 16, smaller = (25 - 7) / 2 = **9**. The numbers are 16 and 9.
:::

## Quadratic equations

A quadratic looks like ax squared + bx + c = 0 and has two roots.

:::example Factorising
Solve x squared - 5x + 6 = 0.

**Solution.** Find two numbers with product 6 and sum -5: -2 and -3. So (x - 2)(x - 3) = 0, and x = **2 or 3**.
:::

:::formula
Quadratic formula: x = (-b plus or minus sqrt(b squared - 4ac)) / 2a
Sum of roots = -b / a        Product of roots = c / a
Discriminant D = b squared - 4ac:  D > 0 two real roots, D = 0 equal roots, D < 0 no real roots
:::

:::example Using sum and product
Two numbers have sum 25 and product 144. Find them.

**Solution.** They are roots of t squared - 25t + 144 = 0. Factorise: (t - 9)(t - 16) = 0, so the numbers are **9 and 16**.
:::

:::example Roots without solving
For x squared - 7x + 12 = 0, what are the sum and the product of the roots?

**Solution.** Sum = 7 and product = **12** (indeed the roots are 3 and 4).
:::

## Inequalities

Treat them like equations, with one special rule: **multiplying or dividing by a negative number reverses the sign.**

:::example
Solve 2x - 3 > 7.

**Solution.** 2x > 10, so x > **5**.
:::

:::example Negative coefficient
Solve -3x < 12.

**Solution.** Divide by -3 and reverse the sign: x > **-4**.
:::

## Checking with options

In multiple-choice tests, you can often **substitute the options** into the equation instead of solving it. Start with the middle option.

:::trick
For x squared + bx + c = 0 with whole-number roots, list the factor pairs of c and pick the pair that adds up to b (with signs).
:::
