## The vocabulary

| Statement | Relation |
| --- | --- |
| Mother's or father's brother | uncle |
| Mother's or father's sister | aunt |
| Mother's mother or father's mother | grandmother |
| Son's wife | daughter-in-law |
| Daughter's husband | son-in-law |
| Sister's husband or wife's brother | brother-in-law |
| Uncle's or aunt's child | cousin |
| Brother's son | nephew |
| Brother's or sister's daughter | niece |

A **paternal** relation is on the father's side. A **maternal** relation is on the mother's side.

## Draw it

A small tree beats guessing. Use a box for a male, a circle for a female, a line down for a child and a line across for a spouse or sibling. Even a rough sketch on rough paper avoids most mistakes.

## Examples

:::example A chain of relations
A is B's sister. C is B's mother. D is C's father. How is A related to D?

**Solution.** A is B's sister, so A is also C's daughter. D is C's father, so D is A's **grandfather**, and A is D's granddaughter.
:::

:::example A photograph
Pointing to a man in a photograph, Rita says, "He is the son of my mother's only brother." How is the man related to Rita?

**Solution.** Rita's mother's brother is Rita's maternal uncle. His son is Rita's **cousin**.
:::

:::example A tricky one
Pointing to a photograph, a man says, "I have no brother or sister, but that man's father is my father's son." Whose photograph is it?

**Solution.** "My father's son" is the speaker himself (he has no brother). So that man's father is the speaker, and the photograph is of the speaker's **son**.
:::

:::example Cousins
X's mother is the sister of Y's father. How is Y related to X?

**Solution.** Y's father's sister is X's mother. So X and Y are children of a brother and a sister, which makes them **cousins**.
:::

## A method that always works

1. Write down each statement as a link between two people.
2. Replace pronouns ("he", "she") with names as soon as you know them.
3. Mark gender where it is stated, and leave it open where it is not.
4. If a person's gender is not given, the question usually asks for something that does not depend on it.

:::tip
"My mother's only brother" and "my uncle" usually mean the same person. Read for words like *only*, they remove ambiguity.
:::

:::remember
"Son" and "daughter" give you the gender. "Child", "cousin" and "sibling" do not. Do not assume.
:::
