takeaways:
- A conclusion follows only if it is certain from the statement alone
- Ignore outside knowledge; use only what is written
- Words like all, some, none, always, only need extra care

## The golden rule

Accept the statements as **true**, even if they are untrue in real life. A conclusion **follows** only if it must be true given the statements. If it is merely possible, it does **not** follow.

## Statement and conclusions

:::example
**Statement:** All doctors are educated. Some educated people are rich.

**Conclusions:**
I. Some doctors are rich.
II. All doctors are educated.

**Solution.** I does not follow: the rich educated people may or may not be doctors. II is given directly, so it follows. Answer: **only II follows**.
:::

:::example
**Statement:** Some pens are books. All books are tables.

**Conclusions:**
I. Some pens are tables.
II. All tables are pens.

**Solution.** The pens that are books are tables, so I follows. II does not (tables could be many other things). Answer: **only I follows**.
:::

## Statement and assumptions

An **assumption** is something the speaker takes for granted for the statement to make sense.

:::example
**Statement:** "Buy a smartphone from this store now, as prices will rise next month."

**Assumptions:**
I. Prices of smartphones may go up next month.
II. The store sells only smartphones.

**Solution.** I is taken for granted, otherwise the advice makes no sense. II is not needed. Answer: **only I is implicit**.
:::

## Statement and course of action

A good **course of action** is practical, directly solves the problem and is not extreme.

:::example
**Statement:** Many students fail in the mathematics paper.

**Courses of action:**
I. Extra classes should be arranged for weak students.
II. The mathematics paper should be removed from the syllabus.

**Solution.** I is practical and helps. II is an extreme step that avoids the problem. Answer: **only I follows**.
:::

## Strong and weak arguments

A **strong** argument is important and relevant to the question. A **weak** argument is trivial, based on opinion or on an example.

## Quick rules

| Words in a conclusion | Careful check |
| --- | --- |
| **All** | needs a definite statement covering every case |
| **Some** | at least one; "some" does **not** mean "not all" |
| **Only / always / never** | usually too strong to follow |
| **Probably / may** | usually acceptable if reasonable |

:::trick
Test a conclusion by looking for a counter-example where the statement is true but the conclusion is false. If you find one, it does not follow.
:::
