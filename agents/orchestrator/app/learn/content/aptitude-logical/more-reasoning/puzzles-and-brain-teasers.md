summary: Classic logic puzzles that test careful thinking: weighing balls, crossing a river, ropes and switches.
takeaways:
- Break the puzzle into small, testable steps
- Use a table or diagram, not memory
- Many puzzles are solved by splitting into groups or working backwards

## 1. The heavy ball

**Puzzle.** There are 8 identical-looking balls and one is slightly heavier. You have a balance scale. Find the heavy ball in just **2 weighings**.

**Solution.** Split into groups of 3, 3 and 2.
1. Weigh 3 against 3. If one side is heavier, the heavy ball is in that group of 3; if they balance, it is in the remaining 2.
2. From a group of 3, weigh any 2 against each other: the heavier one is the answer, and if they balance the third is the answer. From the group of 2, weigh them against each other.

Splitting into **three groups**, not two, is the trick.

## 2. Crossing a river

**Puzzle.** A farmer must take a wolf, a goat and a cabbage across a river. His boat carries only him and one item. Left alone, the wolf eats the goat and the goat eats the cabbage. How does he cross?

**Solution.**
1. Take the **goat** across and come back alone.
2. Take the **wolf** across and bring the **goat** back.
3. Take the **cabbage** across and come back alone.
4. Take the **goat** across.

## 3. Three switches

**Puzzle.** Three switches outside a room control three bulbs inside. You may enter the room only once. How do you find which switch controls which bulb?

**Solution.** Turn switch 1 on for a few minutes, then turn it off. Turn switch 2 on and go in. The bulb that is **on** is switch 2, the bulb that is **warm but off** is switch 1, and the **cold, off** bulb is switch 3.

## 4. Burning ropes

**Puzzle.** You have two ropes, each burns for exactly 60 minutes, but unevenly. Measure 45 minutes.

**Solution.** Light **rope 1 at both ends** and **rope 2 at one end** at the same time. Rope 1 burns out in 30 minutes. At that moment light the other end of rope 2. Its remaining 30 minutes now burn in **15 minutes**. Total: **45 minutes**.

## 5. Which door

**Puzzle.** There are two doors, one to freedom and one to danger, and two guards: one always tells the truth and one always lies. You do not know which is which. You may ask one question to one guard. What do you ask?

**Solution.** Ask either guard: "**What would the other guard say is the safe door?**" Then choose the **opposite** door. The truthful guard honestly reports the liar's wrong answer, and the liar lies about the truthful guard's right answer, so either way the answer points to the wrong door.

## 6. Handshakes

**Puzzle.** At a meeting each of 8 people shakes hands once with every other person. How many handshakes are there?

**Solution.** Number of pairs = 8 x 7 / 2 = **28**.

## 7. The missing rupee

**Puzzle.** Three friends pay 30 rupees for a room. The clerk refunds 5 rupees, and the boy keeps 2 and gives each friend 1 back. Each friend paid 9, so 27 in total, plus 2 with the boy makes 29. Where is the other rupee?

**Solution.** There is no missing rupee. The 27 rupees the friends paid already **includes** the boy's 2 rupees (25 for the room + 2 kept by the boy = 27). Adding the 2 again is the mistake. The correct accounting is 25 (room) + 2 (boy) + 3 (returned to the friends) = **30**.

:::tip
For tricky puzzles, write each step as a line on paper. The error or the solution usually shows itself.
:::
