title: Cause and effect, assertion and reason
summary: Decide how two statements are related, and whether an assertion and its reason are true and connected.
takeaways:
- A cause comes first and produces the effect
- Two effects can come from one cause, or be independent
- For assertion and reason, check both statements and then the link
---
## Cause and effect

Two statements, I and II, describe events. Decide the relationship.

| Case | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| I is the cause, II is the effect | I made II happen |
| II is the cause, I is the effect | II made I happen |
| Both are effects of a common cause | the same reason produced both |
| Both are independent causes | each is a separate reason |
| Both are independent, unrelated | no connection |

:::example Effect
I. The roads were flooded this morning.
II. Heavy rain fell throughout the night.

**Solution.** The rain caused the flooding. **II is the cause and I is the effect.**
:::

:::example Common cause
I. The prices of vegetables have gone up.
II. Many farmers lost crops due to drought this year.

**Solution.** The drought (II) reduced the supply, which raised prices (I). **II is the cause, I is the effect.**
:::

:::example Two effects
I. Schools in the region were closed for a week.
II. Offices in the city asked staff to work from home.

**Solution.** Both can be effects of one cause (such as a storm warning or an outbreak), but neither causes the other. **Both are effects of a common cause** is the best answer.
:::

## Assertion and reason

You get an **Assertion (A)** and a **Reason (R)**. Check:

1. Is A true?
2. Is R true?
3. If both are true, does R **explain** A?

| Option | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Both true, R explains A | correct reason |
| Both true, R does not explain A | true but unrelated |
| A true, R false | |
| A false, R true | |
| Both false | |

:::example
**A:** The sky looks blue.
**R:** Blue light is scattered more by the atmosphere than red light.

**Solution.** Both are true and R explains A. **Both true, R is the correct explanation.**
:::

:::example
**A:** Water boils at 100 degrees Celsius at sea level.
**R:** Water is a liquid.

**Solution.** Both are true, but R does not explain why water boils at 100 degrees. **Both true, R is not the explanation.**
:::

:::trick
Ask "does R answer the question WHY for A?" If you can say "A because R" and it sounds right, R is the explanation.
:::
