## Your reputation is your career

In a new workplace people form an opinion of you in the first weeks. They remember whether you were **reliable, respectful, and willing to learn**. Skills can be taught; attitude is hard to change.

## The first weeks at a job

| Do | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| **Be on time**, every day | arrive 10 minutes early; for meetings, 5 |
| **Learn names and roles** | write them down; use them |
| **Ask questions early** | people expect it; wrong assumptions cost more |
| **Take notes** | do not ask the same thing twice |
| **Observe the culture** | how people dress, speak, share lunch, use chat |
| **Find a buddy or mentor** | someone to ask small questions |
| **Do small tasks well** | doing the basics with care earns bigger tasks |
| **Be proactive** | "I have finished this; is there something else I can help with?" |
| **Ask for feedback** at 2 weeks and 2 months | "What should I do more of, or differently?" |

## Everyday professional behaviour

| Area | Good practice |
| --- | --- |
| **Punctuality** | on time to work, meetings and deadlines; tell people early if you will be late |
| **Dress** | follow the dress code; clean, ironed, modest and comfortable |
| **Language** | polite words: please, thank you, excuse me; no abusive language, even as jokes |
| **Respect for everyone** | the same courtesy to the security guard, the cleaner and the CEO |
| **Phone use** | silent in meetings; personal calls away from colleagues |
| **Desk and shared spaces** | tidy; clean up in the pantry; do not take others' food |
| **Meetings and calls** | prepared, focused, no side conversations |
| **Replies** | acknowledge emails and messages within a working day |
| **Promises** | do what you said; if you cannot, tell people early |

## Respectful and inclusive behaviour

Workplaces include people of different ages, genders, languages, regions, religions and abilities.

| Do | Do not |
| --- | --- |
| treat everyone with respect, whatever their background | make jokes about gender, caste, religion, region, appearance or disability |
| use inclusive language | assume roles by gender ("the manager... he") |
| listen to different views | talk over juniors or women in meetings |
| speak up or report if you see harassment | stay silent when someone is being bullied |

Sexual harassment, discrimination and bullying are serious offences. Companies and colleges have **committees** (an Internal Complaints Committee in India) where you can report safely.

## Confidentiality and ethics

| Principle | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Confidentiality** | do not share client data, company plans, source code or salaries outside the company |
| **Data protection** | do not copy customer data to personal devices or public tools; follow the company's rules on passwords and security |
| **Honesty** | report accurate numbers; admit mistakes early |
| **Conflict of interest** | tell your manager if a personal interest could affect your work |
| **Gifts and favours** | follow the company policy; do not accept anything that may influence your decision |
| **Credit** | give credit for others' work; do not copy and claim as your own |
| **Company property** | use the laptop, internet and software for work, as the policy says |

:::warning
Never paste confidential company code, customer data or internal documents into public websites or AI tools without permission. Many companies have strict rules, and breaking them can end a career.
:::

## Social media and your job

| Do | Do not |
| --- | --- |
| keep your public posts respectful | post complaints about your boss, colleagues or clients |
| check privacy settings | share photos of confidential documents or office screens |
| represent the company only if authorised | give "official" opinions for the company on your personal account |
| think before you post: would you say this at an interview? | argue angrily online |

## Dealing with mistakes at work

1. **Tell** the right person quickly. Hiding a mistake makes it worse.
2. **Fix** what you can.
3. **Explain** what happened briefly and without blaming others.
4. **Prevent** it: what will you do differently?
5. **Move on.** Everyone makes mistakes; managers remember how you handled them.

:::example Reporting a mistake
"Hi Mr Rao, I want to let you know that I sent the draft report to the wrong client list this morning. I have already recalled the email and informed the three people who received it. From now on, I will double-check the recipient list before sending. I am sorry for the error."
:::

## Growing in your job

| Habit | Why |
| --- | --- |
| **keep learning** (courses, reading, side projects) | your skills stay current |
| **find a mentor** | advice and guidance |
| **keep a record of achievements** | useful for reviews and resumes |
| **build relationships** across teams | helps in projects and promotions |
| **ask for stretch tasks** | growth comes from challenge |
| **think about your next step** | where do you want to be in two years? |

## Work-life balance and burnout

| Sign of trouble | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| constant tiredness, dread of work, irritability | talk to someone: a friend, family, a manager, a counsellor |
| working late every night | discuss workload; prioritise; set limits |
| no time for health or family | schedule it like a meeting |

Long hours can look dedicated, but burnout reduces quality. Work **sustainably**.

## A quick professionalism checklist

1. Am I on time and reliable?
2. Do I communicate early about problems?
3. Do I treat everyone respectfully?
4. Do I keep confidential things confidential?
5. Do I own my mistakes and learn from them?
6. Do I keep learning and ask for feedback?
