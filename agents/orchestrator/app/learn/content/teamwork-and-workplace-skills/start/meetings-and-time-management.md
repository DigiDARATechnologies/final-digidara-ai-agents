## Attending meetings

| Before | During | After |
| --- | --- | --- |
| read the agenda and any documents | be on time; phone on silent | do the actions you promised |
| prepare your updates and questions | listen; take notes | send thanks or a short summary if you were the organiser |
| decide what you want to achieve | contribute briefly and clearly | follow up on open items |

Speak up. If you have an idea or a concern and say nothing, the team cannot use it. Be concise: "My update: login is done. I need help with the payment keys."

## Running a meeting

| Element | How |
| --- | --- |
| **Purpose** | decide if a meeting is needed at all: an email or a short chat may do |
| **Invite the right people** | only those who must be there |
| **Agenda** | send it beforehand with the time for each item |
| **Time** | start and finish on time; 15 or 30 minutes is often enough |
| **Roles** | a time-keeper and a note-taker |
| **Everyone speaks** | draw in quieter people |
| **Decisions and actions** | at the end, state: decisions, owner, deadline |
| **Notes** | send within 24 hours |

:::example Meeting agenda
**Weekly project check-in: Mon 10:00 to 10:30**
1. Updates from each member (10 minutes)
2. Blockers (10 minutes)
3. Plan for next week (5 minutes)
4. Action items (5 minutes)
:::

## Time management basics

You cannot add hours to the day, but you can choose how to use them.

### The Eisenhower matrix

Sort tasks by **urgency** and **importance**:

| | Urgent | Not urgent |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Important** | **Do now** (deadline tomorrow, a client problem) | **Schedule** (learning a skill, exam preparation, health) |
| **Not important** | **Delegate or do quickly** (some emails, most interruptions) | **Reduce or delete** (endless scrolling, trivial chats) |

Most success comes from the **important but not urgent** box: prepare for the exam **before** the last week, learn the skill **before** the interview.

### Plan your day

| Habit | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| **List tomorrow's tasks the evening before** | you start with a plan |
| **Pick the 3 most important tasks** (MITs) | do them first |
| **Estimate time**, then add 25% | we usually underestimate |
| **Time-block** your calendar | "9 to 11 am: project work" |
| **Batch small tasks** | answer messages at set times, not constantly |
| **Leave buffer time** | surprises happen |

### Break big tasks into small steps

"Finish the project" is vague and scary. "Write the login page" is a step you can start.

| Big task | Small steps |
| --- | --- |
| Prepare for the interview | 1. update the resume, 2. write a self-introduction, 3. practise five STAR stories, 4. research the company, 5. do two mock interviews |
| Final-year project | requirements, design, setup, feature 1, feature 2, testing, report, slides |

## Beating procrastination

We delay tasks that feel hard, boring or unclear. Tactics that work:

| Tactic | How |
| --- | --- |
| **Start for just 5 minutes** | starting is the hardest part; momentum follows |
| **Make the first step tiny** | "open the file and write the title" |
| **The Pomodoro technique** | 25 minutes of focused work, then a 5-minute break; after four rounds, a longer break |
| **Remove distractions** | phone in another room, notifications off, a separate study place |
| **Set small deadlines and tell someone** | accountability |
| **Reward yourself** after finishing | a walk, a snack, a favourite episode |
| **Do the hardest task first** (when your energy is highest) | the "eat the frog" idea |

## Focus and digital habits

| Habit | Why |
| --- | --- |
| turn off non-essential notifications | each interruption can take several minutes to recover from |
| check email and messages at set times | reduces constant switching |
| use one task list (paper, a notes app, Notion, Google Tasks) | nothing gets forgotten |
| avoid multitasking | quality drops when switching |
| keep your phone out of sight while studying | its presence alone is distracting |

## Look after yourself

Productivity depends on health.

| Area | Habit |
| --- | --- |
| **Sleep** | 7 to 8 hours; late-night cramming reduces memory and mood |
| **Movement** | a walk or exercise daily |
| **Food and water** | regular meals, not only snacks |
| **Breaks** | a short break every hour; step away from the screen |
| **Social time** | friends and family; do not isolate |
| **Stress** | breathing, talking to someone, hobbies; seek help if stress feels overwhelming (a counsellor, a trusted teacher, a helpline) |

## Handling several deadlines

1. **List** everything with the due dates and how long each will take.
2. **Prioritise** (importance and urgency).
3. **Communicate early** if you cannot do all: "I can deliver A on Tuesday and B on Friday. Which is the priority?"
4. **Work in focused blocks**, not in a panic on the last night.
5. **Review** at the end of each week: what went well, what to change.

## A weekly review (15 minutes)

| Question | |
| --- | --- |
| What did I finish? | celebrate it |
| What did I not finish, and why? | learn from it |
| What are the 3 most important things next week? | plan |
| What distracted me most? | reduce it |

:::tip
Use your calendar for **everything**: classes, deadlines, practice sessions, even rest. Whatever is not in the calendar tends not to happen.
:::
