## Listening is a skill

Most people listen to reply, not to understand. In a workplace, poor listening causes mistakes, repeated work and bad feelings. Good listening makes you trusted.

## Active listening

| Step | What to do | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Pay full attention** | put the phone away, turn towards the speaker | do not look at your laptop |
| **Show you are listening** | nod, eye contact, short responses | "I see." "Go on." |
| **Do not interrupt** | let them finish; note your thought | wait for a pause |
| **Paraphrase** | say it back in your own words | "So you need the report by Thursday, with the new figures." |
| **Ask a clarifying question** | to avoid guessing | "Do you mean last month's data or this month's?" |
| **Notice feelings** | tone and body language also carry meaning | "You sound worried about the deadline." |
| **Write it down** | for tasks, numbers and names | repeat the action points |

## Why asking helps

Smart questions save time and show care. A short clarification at the start is much cheaper than redoing a task.

| Type | Use | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Open** questions (what, how, why) | to learn and explore | "How should the report look?" |
| **Closed** questions (yes or no, a fact) | to confirm | "Is the deadline Friday?" |
| **Probing** questions | to go deeper | "What was the main reason it failed?" |
| **Clarifying** questions | to avoid misunderstanding | "When you say 'soon', do you mean today?" |

Before asking a colleague for help, **try first** for a reasonable time, and explain what you tried: "I checked the documentation and tried X and Y, but I still get this error."

:::example A good request for help
"Hi Anil, I'm stuck on the login test. I have checked the logs and the password reset works, but the new users get an error 500. Here is the screenshot. Do you have 10 minutes this afternoon to take a look?"
:::

## Giving feedback

Done well, feedback helps people improve. Done badly, it hurts and changes nothing.

**The SBI model:** Situation, Behaviour, Impact.

| Part | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Situation** | when and where | "In yesterday's client call..." |
| **Behaviour** | what you observed, facts, not guesses about intention | "...you interrupted Meena twice..." |
| **Impact** | the effect on you, the team or the work | "...and she stopped sharing her ideas." |
| **(Then)** a suggestion or question | what could be better | "Could you let her finish next time?" |

| Do | Do not |
| --- | --- |
| be **specific** | "You are careless." |
| talk about **behaviour**, not personality | "You are lazy." |
| give feedback **soon** after the event | store complaints for months |
| do it **privately** for improvement, publicly for praise | embarrass someone in front of others |
| balance with **genuine positives** | use the "praise sandwich" mechanically |
| ask how they see it | assume you know all the facts |

## Receiving feedback

Feedback is hard to hear, but it is the fastest way to grow.

| Step | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| **Listen** without arguing or defending | breathe; let them finish |
| **Ask for examples** | "Could you show me where it was unclear?" |
| **Say thank you** | even if it stings, they spent effort on you |
| **Think** before replying | take time if needed |
| **Decide what to change** and tell them | "I will use the new template from next week." |
| **Follow up** | "Does this version work better?" |

If feedback is unfair or unclear, say so calmly: "I would like to understand better. Could you give me one or two examples?"

## Praise and recognition

Thank people **specifically**: "Thank you for staying late to fix the bug: it let us demo on time." Specific praise tells people what to repeat.

## Listening in different settings

| Setting | Tips |
| --- | --- |
| **Class or lecture** | take notes in your own words; ask one question per session |
| **Meetings** | note decisions and action items |
| **Phone calls** | do not multitask; repeat important details |
| **Online meetings** | camera on if expected; use chat for short questions; do not type unrelated messages |
| **With a customer** | let them finish the complaint, acknowledge, then solve |

## Common listening mistakes

| Mistake | Better |
| --- | --- |
| planning your reply while they are still speaking | focus on understanding first |
| finishing their sentences | wait |
| checking your phone | put it away |
| giving advice when someone only wants to be heard | ask: "Do you want advice, or should I just listen?" |
| nodding without understanding | paraphrase to check |
