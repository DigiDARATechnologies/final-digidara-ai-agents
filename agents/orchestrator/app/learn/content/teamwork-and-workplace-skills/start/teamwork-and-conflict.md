## What makes a team work

| Ingredient | In practice |
| --- | --- |
| **Shared goal** | everyone knows what success looks like |
| **Clear roles** | who does what, by when |
| **Open communication** | updates, questions and problems are shared early |
| **Trust** | people keep promises and admit mistakes |
| **Respect** | different views and styles are valued |
| **Accountability** | each person owns their part and helps others |

## Be a good team member

| Do | Example |
| --- | --- |
| **keep your promises** | deliver by the date you said, or warn early |
| **communicate early** | "I may be delayed by a day: the data file has errors." |
| **help others** | share what you learned; do not hoard knowledge |
| **give credit** | mention teammates in your presentations |
| **accept your share** (including boring tasks) | volunteer for part of the unglamorous work |
| **respect different working styles** | some think aloud, some need quiet time |
| **be reliable in small things** | on time to meetings, replies to messages |

Teams also have **stages** (forming, storming, norming, performing). Arguments in the "storming" stage are normal. They are the way a team learns to work together.

## Team roles

Groups work better when they notice who is good at what:

| Role | Strength |
| --- | --- |
| **Leader / coordinator** | organises, keeps the goal, resolves issues |
| **Ideas person** | thinks creatively |
| **Finisher** | checks details, completes tasks |
| **Researcher** | finds information and contacts |
| **Presenter** | communicates the results |
| **Peacemaker** | keeps relationships healthy |

You may play several roles. Think about **what the team needs**, not just what you like.

## Handling conflict

Disagreement is normal and can even improve decisions. It becomes a problem when it turns **personal**.

| Style | Description | When useful |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Avoid** | ignore it | only for trivial issues |
| **Give in** | let the other person win | when the issue matters more to them |
| **Compete** | insist on your way | emergencies |
| **Compromise** | each gives a little | time pressure |
| **Collaborate** | find a solution that meets both needs | most work conflicts |

### A step-by-step approach

1. **Stay calm.** If you are angry, take a break before responding.
2. **Talk privately** with the person, not in front of the group, and not about them behind their back.
3. **Describe the issue** with facts: "The report is two days late, and it affects our submission."
4. **Listen** to their side and ask questions.
5. **Focus on the problem**, not blame: "How can we fix this?"
6. **Find a solution** that meets the shared goal.
7. **Agree** on actions, with who and when.
8. **Follow up.**

:::example Handling a conflict
"Arun, can we talk about the testing plan? I noticed that the tests were not done on Tuesday as agreed, and it delayed the release. I would like to understand what happened and see how we can avoid it next time. Is something blocking you?"
:::

This opening is calm, specific, and invites explanation.

## Assertiveness

| Style | What it sounds like | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Passive** | "It's okay, whatever you want." (you stay silent, resent later) | people ignore your needs |
| **Aggressive** | "You always mess this up!" | people become defensive or afraid |
| **Assertive** | "I need the data by Wednesday to finish on time. Can you send it by then?" | clear, respectful, effective |

**Assertive formula:** *"When X happens, I feel Y (or the effect is Y). I would like Z."*

| Situation | Assertive phrase |
| --- | --- |
| a teammate keeps interrupting | "I would like to finish my point, then I am happy to hear yours." |
| you are given too much work | "I can finish A and B this week. If I take C as well, one of them will be late. Which should I prioritise?" |
| someone takes credit for your work | "I would like our presentation to mention that I built the dashboard." |
| you disagree with a plan | "I see it differently: my concern is the timeline. Could we discuss another approach?" |

## Saying no politely

| Step | Example |
| --- | --- |
| **Thank them** | "Thanks for asking me." |
| **Say no clearly** | "I am not able to take this on." |
| **Give a brief reason** (optional) | "I am at full capacity with the client demo." |
| **Offer an alternative** | "I can help next week, or I can show Meena how to do it." |

Saying "yes" to everything leads to missed deadlines and poor quality. A respectful, clear "no" builds trust.

## Working with difficult people

| Type | Approach |
| --- | --- |
| **someone who does not do their part** | talk privately and clarify expectations; if it continues, involve the supervisor or teacher with facts |
| **someone who criticises everything** | ask for specific suggestions: "What would you change?" |
| **someone who dominates** | involve others; set agendas and time limits |
| **someone rude** | stay professional, set a boundary: "I will continue when we can speak respectfully." |

Report **harassment, discrimination or bullying** to a trusted senior, HR or the college's committee. This is not a conflict to handle alone.

:::remember
In a team, you succeed or fail together. Think "How can we get this done?" rather than "Whose fault is this?"
:::
