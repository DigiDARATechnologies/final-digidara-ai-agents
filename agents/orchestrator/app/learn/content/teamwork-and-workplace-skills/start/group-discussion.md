## What evaluators look for

A group discussion (GD) is used in many campus drives and admissions to see how you behave in a group.

| Skill | What it looks like |
| --- | --- |
| **Content** | relevant points, facts, examples |
| **Communication** | clear, confident, simple language; good listening |
| **Structure and logic** | organised ideas, reasons for your opinion |
| **Teamwork** | involving others, respecting views, not dominating |
| **Leadership** | steering the discussion, summarising, resolving conflict |
| **Behaviour** | calm, polite, positive body language |

Most people think they must speak the most. Evaluators prefer someone who **speaks meaningfully**, **listens**, and **helps the group reach a useful place**.

## Types of topics

| Type | Examples | Approach |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Factual** | "India's startup ecosystem", "Digital payments" | facts, trends, numbers |
| **Controversial** | "Work from home is better than the office" | balanced view, reasons, respect |
| **Abstract** | "Blue is better than red", "Zero" | creative, structured interpretation |
| **Case study** | a scenario with a decision to make | analyse, decide, justify |
| **Current affairs** | recent news | background, causes, effects, solutions |

## A simple structure for your points

For any topic, think in dimensions so your points are organised:

| Framework | Use for |
| --- | --- |
| **PEST**: Political, Economic, Social, Technological | national or business topics |
| **Past, Present, Future** | trends |
| **Advantages and disadvantages** | "Is X good?" topics |
| **Individual, Society, Government, Business** | social issues |
| **Causes, Effects, Solutions** | problems |

For each point: **claim, reason, example**.

:::example Structured contribution
Topic: *"Should coaching centres be regulated?"*

"I would like to add a point about student well-being. Many coaching centres run long hours for teenagers, which can affect their health and sleep. For example, news reports from Kota have repeatedly highlighted student stress. So I believe some regulation on hours and counselling is needed, in addition to the fee-related rules that Meena mentioned."
:::

## How to enter and build

| Stage | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| **Starting** | if you have a clear idea, a strong opening is excellent: define the topic, give a short fact, or state the issue. Do not start with a weak or unclear idea just to be first. |
| **Middle** | add distinct points, build on others ("Building on what Arun said..."), give examples, politely disagree with reasons |
| **Closing** | if time is ending, summarise the key points of the discussion, not just your own |

**Phrases that work:**

| Purpose | Phrase |
| --- | --- |
| Enter | "May I add a point here?" / "I would like to build on that." |
| Agree | "I agree with Meena, and I would add that..." |
| Disagree | "I see your point, but have we considered..." |
| Involve a silent person | "Priya, we have not heard your view. What do you think?" |
| Clarify | "If I understand correctly, you mean..." |
| Redirect | "That is interesting; let us return to the main question of..." |
| Summarise | "To sum up, we discussed three points: ..." |

## Dealing with different participants

| Participant | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| **The dominant speaker** | do not shout; politely say "Let me add a point" at a pause; stay calm; a strong, brief point works |
| **The silent member** | invite them in kindly: it shows leadership |
| **A person who argues aggressively** | stay respectful; use facts; do not match the tone |
| **A person who goes off-topic** | gently bring it back: "That is a good point; how does it relate to...?" |
| **Chaos (everyone talking)** | wait for a short gap and speak slowly and firmly; a calm voice stands out |

## Do and do not

| Do | Do not |
| --- | --- |
| listen carefully and refer to others' points by name | interrupt, shout, or talk over others |
| stay on the topic | go off into personal stories or jokes |
| use facts and examples | give only opinions ("I feel...") |
| keep each point to **20 to 30 seconds** | speak for two minutes at once |
| be polite even when you disagree | insult or mock |
| keep a positive, steady body language | slouch, point, or look at the floor |
| speak 2 to 4 times meaningfully | speak only once, or speak constantly |
| admit when someone makes a better point | defend a weak argument stubbornly |

## Preparing

| How | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| **Read** a newspaper or a news app daily for 20 minutes | gives you facts |
| **Keep a notebook** of 20 current topics with 3 facts, 3 arguments and 1 example each | quick revision |
| **Practise** in groups with friends, with a timer, and give each other feedback | the best method |
| **Record** a practice GD and watch your body language | self-awareness |
| **Learn** common facts: GDP, population, Digital India, startups, climate, AI | background |

## A sample topic outline

**Topic:** "Social media: boon or bane?"

| Dimension | Boon | Bane |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Individual | learning, skills, finding jobs | addiction, comparison, anxiety |
| Society | awareness, communities, small business | misinformation, cyberbullying |
| Economy | creators, digital marketing, new jobs | scams, privacy loss |
| Way forward | digital literacy, platform responsibility, time limits | |

A strong closing summary: "The group agrees that social media is a tool: its effect depends on how we use it, so digital literacy and healthy limits are the way forward."

:::tip
Evaluators remember the person who **improved the discussion**: someone who clarified a confusing point, brought the group back to the topic, or summarised well, even if they spoke less than others.
:::

:::practice communication-coach
Pick a current topic, write three structured points (claim, reason, example), and practise saying each in 30 seconds.
:::
